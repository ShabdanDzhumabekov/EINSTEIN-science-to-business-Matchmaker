# Serbian Morphology and Script Canonicalisation for LinguaBot Search

## Verified facts
- The company reports that in a sample of 12,000 real support queries from one bank client, about 18% returned no useful result, with more than half of those misses traced to morphological mismatch or script confusion. [F1]
- The company's good outcome is getting that 18% miss rate below 5% and keeping it there with a manageable maintenance process. [F2]
- The company states a budget of around 8,000 EUR for tooling or data work and wants something testable within six months. [F3]
- The company requires an approach that tolerates script mixing automatically and can be updated when new vocabulary arrives without a full re-index. [F4]
- The company has around 85,000 labelled query-response pairs across clients, and two in-house linguists who can annotate or validate solutions. [F5]
- The researcher's group works on mixed Cyrillic–Latin Serbian retrieval, script normalisation, transliteration preprocessing and script-robust inverted indexes; his notes state capacity for one smaller industrial contract. [F6]
- The researcher's published method uses a phoneme-based canonical token form as the primary index identifier, reporting F1 0.81 in ad-hoc search, 14 points above the better of two baselines. [F7]
- The researcher's morphological paper reports stemming after normalisation gives consistently better results for all three combinations, evaluated on a CLEF 2012 subset with both scripts. [F8]

## Our inference
- Transfer is untested: the researcher's evidence comes from news, legal, encyclopedia-style and web corpora, while LinguaBot queries include mid-word script typos and bank support vocabulary.
- Manual dictionary upkeep is likely a risk: the researcher's pilot paper notes modules need updating for new terminology, and the company's synonym-list experience points the same way.
- The researcher's latency study reports a cost for neural normalisation and lower cost for a cascaded OOV fallback; production SLOs are unknown, so neural components should be optional.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment (months 1–2): test on the 12,000-query miss sample, with the 85,000 labelled pairs as held-out validation. Baseline: current Elasticsearch pipeline with the Croatian stemmer. Run four variants on identical queries: script canonicalisation only, Serbian lemmatisation/stemming only, stem-after-normalisation, and deterministic normalisation with neural fallback only for out-of-vocabulary tokens. Change one suspected factor at a time. Measure the company's 'no useful result' rate; add MRR@10 and P@5 if labels allow. Proposed go/no-go: proceed only if the best variant lowers no-useful-result by at least 5 absolute points versus baseline and stem-after-normalisation beats stem-only by a pre-registered margin; the below-5% target is tested in the six-month pilot, not assumed here. Proposed contributions: researcher supplies canonicalisation/stemming code and analysis; company supplies queries, labels, Elasticsearch config and linguist validation.
- If it passes, build an Elasticsearch integration prototype for new product terminology using override rules plus an incremental update path, and test new-vocabulary queries without full re-indexing (months 3–4).
- Run a held-out client pilot with linguist validation; proposed success criteria are below 5% no-useful-result rate and no P@5 degradation (months 5–6, subject to budget and availability).
- Measure production latency and throughput against SLOs to be confirmed with the company, comparing deterministic-only and OOV neural fallback; do not claim hardware fit from model reduction alone.
