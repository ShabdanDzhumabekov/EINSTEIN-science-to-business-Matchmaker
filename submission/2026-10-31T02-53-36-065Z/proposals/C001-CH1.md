# Per-site unsupervised ICS baselines and calibration for Modbus/BACnet on ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec states it has no confirmed attack capture from clients and only normal-traffic archives collected over three years. [F1]
- The company’s ProtoSense product is specified as passive protocol inspection for Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP traffic. [F2]
- A previous supervised classifier, fed public ICS data, produced so many false positives in real deployments that clients disabled alerts. [F3]
- The company requires on-device anomaly detection without affecting packet forwarding latency. [F4]
- The company requires alerts that can be explained to a plant manager in plain language. [F5]
- ArmaturaSec can dedicate one to two engineers part-time and give access to traffic archives, hardware bench, and firmware codebase under NDA. [F6]
- Kiss is open to joint projects with companies deploying ICS monitoring solutions and to contract research on anomaly-detection deployment. [F7]
- Kiss’s published work includes per-register Modbus/TCP baselines, a one-class Mahalanobis detector, and post-hoc threshold calibration using unlabelled operational data. [F8]

## Our inference
- Kiss’s documented testbed lists Modbus/TCP and DNP3 generators, not BACnet/IP; this suggests BACnet samples for the first experiment must come from ArmaturaSec archives or a new generator, and transfer remains to be tested.
- Her per-register baselines and post-hoc calibration are likely relevant to no-label, per-site detection, but the prior alert-fatigue failure means false-alarm acceptance, not detection accuracy alone, is the main operational risk.
- Kiss’s availability is unknown in the material, so the joint-project timeline is conditional; her openness to the format is not a confirmed commitment.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment (proposed 12 weeks): use held-out normal captures from ArmaturaSec archives for one Modbus TCP site and one BACnet/IP site (BACnet capture availability to be confirmed; Kiss’s documented testbed is Modbus/DNP3, so do not assume it supplies BACnet). Train a per-site baseline on an early normal window and hold out later normal windows; compare against a fixed-threshold rule and a static Gaussian baseline. Kiss proposes feature and calibration design and analysis; ArmaturaSec proposes archive access, bench or mirrored segment, firmware integration, and one part-time engineer. Inject controlled anomalies one factor at a time. Proposed go/no-go targets: ≤1 false alarm per site per day on held-out normal; ≥80% recall on injected anomalies; no packet loss and p99 forwarding-latency increase within an agreed bound; an ArmaturaSec-nominated plant-facing reviewer can explain each alert card unaided, with pilot plant-manager testing only if consenting clients are secured; BACnet no-go if no BACnet normal sample is available.
- If the first experiment passes, run a calibration and drift study on ArmaturaSec multi-site normal archives: compare static versus incremental updates and measure seasonal or process-change false alarms, testing whether Kiss’s testbed-calibrated recalibration transfers to company traffic.
- Port the selected detector to the agreed ProtoSense or NetGuard target under NDA and measure CPU, memory, and forwarding latency on the hardware bench; if the planned security-processor variant is unavailable, use the existing appliance as baseline.
- Conditional on ArmaturaSec securing two consenting pilot clients, co-design an alert card with their plant managers and measure whether they can judge an alert actionable unaided; otherwise use ArmaturaSec engineer review and defer the plant-manager test.
