# Site-specific, explainable anomaly detection for ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec reports several terabytes of captures collected from consenting clients over three years, but nothing labelled as malicious. [F1]
- ProtoSense inspects Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP. ArmaturaSec reports that excessive false positives caused clients to disable alerts. [F2]
- The company uses embedded Linux and reports having hardware for on-device inference. Its hardware-variant plan links a dedicated security processor with on-device anomaly detection and packet-forwarding latency. [F3]
- The company's collaboration-access statement names archives, hardware bench, firmware and an NDA. Kiss's testbed-access statement names a data-sharing agreement. [F4]
- ArmaturaSec offers one to two part-time engineers. Kiss lists openness to joint projects with companies deploying ICS monitoring solutions. [F5]
- Kiss's Modbus work estimates register-level timing, payload and command-sequence distributions. Her incremental modelling work reports planned process reconfiguration as a persistent anomaly episode. [F6]
- Her Isolation Forest study reports a calibration risk: a tenfold contamination-estimate mismatch reduced average precision from 0.91 to 0.63. Her embedded experiments used Modbus/TCP and DNP3 captures. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could establish whether site-specific detection offers manageable, understandable alerts on both protocols without compromising appliance operation, before wider deployment.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permissions, Kiss could test how calibration and guarded adaptation transfer to BACnet and heterogeneous client operations, using operational captures alongside controlled bench evidence.

## Our inference
- This suggests a practical joint project around interpretable site-specific baselines and guarded incremental updates. The proposal is conditional because Kiss's availability is unknown; openness to joint projects does not establish a time commitment.
- BACnet behaviour, actual appliance contention and plant-manager understanding remain transfer gaps. Modbus/DNP3 results and previous processor measurements do not establish performance on ProtoSense.
- The archives should be treated as presumed normal, not ground truth. Undetected compromise, maintenance and seasonality could distort baselines; adaptation could absorb persistent attacks unless operator-approved updates and rollback are tested.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose separate training, tuning and held-out client periods; verify temporal independence and operating-mode coverage. Train/calibrate without attack labels. Compare fixed rules, statistical site baselines and an incremental detector; vary contamination and update safeguards individually. Freeze label-free tuning: lowest alert-episode rate subject to agreed resource and baseline-sensitivity constraints, ties favouring simplicity. Evaluate the selected configuration separately for Modbus and BACnet on held-out traffic and isolated-bench injections, including persistent anomalies and process changes. Measure recall, delay, false-alert burden, comprehension, CPU, RAM and forwarding latency. Proposed go/no-go, separately for each protocol: at least 80% of injected episodes detected within 60 seconds; 95% upper confidence bound at most one false episode per fully monitored site-day with that protocol present; at least 80% correct plant-manager interpretation tasks. Require protocol-specific 95% lower confidence bounds of at least 80% for timely recall and comprehension, using predefined matching, tasks and denominators. Require hardware-budget compliance and no forwarding-latency regression beyond agreed measurement tolerance under representative load. Missing data, insufficient evidence or unsafe testing means no pass, regardless of selected method.
- Propose merging same-site, asset, anomaly-type alerts separated by at most five minutes into one episode. Experts review every episode; unresolved episodes count as false. Separately per protocol, divide false episodes by fully monitored site-days with that protocol present; account for temporal clustering in confidence bounds. Randomly sample all evaluation windows, alerted or not, with equal recorded inclusion probabilities for expert labelling. Window labels assess contamination, not episode-rate numerators; detection claims use only injected or confirmed cases.
- Propose six weeks after permissions and staffing confirmation: two for access, coverage and splits; two for replay and safe bench scenarios; two for held-out testing and hardware measurements. Kiss would lead features, calibration and analysis; company engineers support parsing, integration and operator review within part-time capacity. Establish NDA, research-sharing permission and testbed agreements first; collection consent alone is insufficient. Freeze updates during evaluation, separately test operator-approved resets, and document integration, rollback and maintenance ownership.

## Open questions
- Can Kiss confirm availability for this joint project, and can both sides confirm staffing, funding and the proposed schedule?
- Which appliance and security processor are available, and what CPU, memory, traffic-load and forwarding-latency budgets should govern acceptance?
- Do permissions cover research sharing and replay, and are representative BACnet captures, operating logs, safely reproducible scenarios and plant-manager participants available?
- Before testing, can both sides approve one-to-one injection-to-alert matching by asset/type from injection onset, protocol-specific sample sizes and confidence procedures, and representative interpretation tasks covering affected equipment, deviation and appropriate response, with a correctness rubric and all assigned tasks as denominator, alongside alert adjudication, tuning constraints, update approvals and maintenance ownership?
