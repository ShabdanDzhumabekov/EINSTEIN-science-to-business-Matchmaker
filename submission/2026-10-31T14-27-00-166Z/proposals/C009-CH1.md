# Joint project: reducing Serbian support-search misses

## Verified facts
- LinguaBot reports that about 18% of a sample of 12,000 real support queries from one bank client returned no useful result; its analysis attributed more than half of those misses to morphology or script confusion. [F1]
- The company’s Elasticsearch setup uses a Croatian-oriented stemmer, while users mix Latin and Cyrillic even within words. [F2]
- LinguaBot reports that manually maintaining synonym lists does not scale as product terminology changes quarterly. [F3]
- The company states a tooling/data-work budget of around EUR 8,000. [F4]
- LinguaBot describes around 85,000 labelled query-response pairs across all clients and two in-house linguists who can annotate or validate solutions. [F5]
- Nikolić’s work describes cross-script lemmatization and normalization inside tokens. Her participation record lists openness to joint projects. [F6]
- Her morphological-lexicon study reports lower paradigm correctness for borrowings than for common vocabulary: 78.6% versus 91.3%. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could identify which components reduce support-search misses and whether vocabulary updates remain practical without full re-indexing.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permissions, support-domain evaluation material could enable research on cross-script morphology, borrowing errors and the gap between linguistic accuracy and retrieval usefulness.

## Our inference
- This suggests methodological fit, but corpus and social-media results do not establish support-retrieval performance. Banking and telecom terminology and within-word typos need direct testing; borrowing errors and meaning conflation could increase false matches.
- Query-time normalization and expansion could offer an incremental-update route, but Elasticsearch compatibility and avoidance of full re-indexing remain unvalidated.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional because Nikolić’s availability is unknown. Openness to joint projects does not establish a time commitment, company staffing or an agreed total budget.

## Proposed next steps
- Use permitted query-response pairs and knowledge-base documents, separating tuning from held-out evaluation; verify independence through duplicate, client and time checks. Compare current Elasticsearch against an eight-configuration matrix: baseline, Serbian lemmatization, within-token normalization, paradigm expansion, each pair and all three. Define factor settings independently; hold documents and ranking settings constant. Select the best configuration on tuning data, whichever it is. Linguists would judge every evaluation query, including non-misses, using a fixed usefulness rubric. Count each query once: miss rate is queries without useful results divided by all evaluated queries; unresolved judgments count as misses. Proposed go/no-go requires its upper 95% confidence bound below 5% on a separate held-out sample from the original bank population, plus precision non-loss under the pre-agreed rule. Require separate acceptance for telecom, mixed-script and unseen-term populations, not merely reporting. Missing evaluation data or insufficient evidence prevents a pass. Update, maintenance, budget and six-month feasibility gates also apply.
- Propose month one for access, rubric and architecture checks, months two–four for tuning, and months five–six for evaluation. Pre-agree vocabulary-update rounds and independent post-update evaluation exposure; require simultaneous 95% upper bank miss-rate bounds below 5% in every round. Updates must avoid full re-indexing and continually expanding manual synonym lists. Measure index operations, update time and linguist hours; require an agreed maintenance-hours ceiling, tooling/data costs within approximately EUR 8,000 and a testable prototype within six months.
- Subject to agreement, Nikolić would guide morphology, normalization and error analysis; LinguaBot engineers would implement comparisons, and its linguists would validate relevance and paradigms within reserved hours. Before implementation, establish protected data access, usable commercial licences, deployment limits, permitted initial index changes and maintenance ownership. Record latency, precision and vocabulary-validation effort alongside retrieval gains; test incremental updates early rather than assuming published tools support them.

## Open questions
- Can Nikolić confirm availability for this six-month joint project, with reserved company engineering and linguist hours, and clarify total-cost allocation including researcher compensation?
- What client permissions, anonymization, retention, external-service restrictions and resource licences govern queries, responses and documents, and can LinguaBot reproduce its original usefulness rubric and provide independent bank evaluation data?
- Before tuning, can both sides agree precision at a fixed retrieval cutoff, defining the relevant-result count, returned-slot denominator, query-level averaging and treatment of empty returns, plus a paired uncertainty-based no-loss rule—for example, a nonnegative lower 95% confidence bound on the mean precision difference—with unresolved relevance judged conservatively and insufficient evidence preventing acceptance?
- Before tuning, can both sides agree separate acceptance thresholds, confidence rules and sufficient independent evaluation exposure for telecom, mixed-script and unseen-term populations using the same miss-rate definition, alongside the update-round sequence, maintenance-hours ceiling and deployment limits, treating unavailable evidence as an unresolved prerequisite rather than a pass?
