# Investigating cold-store conveyor weld cracking

## Verified facts
- AutomVoj describes painted mild-steel hollow-section frames with cracking at the main crossmember-to-stringer connections. [F1]
- The company reports failures after roughly 18 months in cold stores typically operating at 2–6 °C, 14 warranty replacements this year, and unsuccessful reproduction during room-temperature load tests. [F2]
- AutomVoj suspects interacting thermal cycling, condensation and loaded-belt vibration. Its existing evidence includes site photographs, measurements and two retained failed sections. [F3]
- AutomVoj lacks cold cyclic-load testing capability. Erić’s group uses fatigue machines and a climate chamber, but the chamber and servo-hydraulic machine are shared with two other groups. [F4]
- AutomVoj has no dedicated R&D department; its chief engineer and two senior automation engineers divide their time between live projects and improvement work. [F5]
- Erić’s published work tested combined thermal and mechanical fatigue in butt-welded S275JR plates. Out-of-phase loading shortened initiation life, and combined-loading crack growth exceeded simple-superposition predictions. [F6]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could explain why workshop tests miss field cracking and identify which contributors warrant manufacturing or design trials, without prematurely attributing failure to cold temperature alone.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and access, Erić could test whether combined-loading fatigue methods transfer to painted conveyor joints, using retained failure evidence, service histories and controlled comparison specimens.

## Our inference
- Erić’s combined-loading methods suggest a suitable starting point, not validated conveyor-life predictions. Different steel, painted hollow-section geometry and milder temperatures create a transfer gap; condensation or coating damage could require additional corrosion expertise.
- Two retained sections may not represent all replacements. Missing service histories and acceleration-induced changes in damage mechanism could prevent a unique explanation or any defensible extrapolation to 18-month service.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional on Erić’s unknown availability and laboratory feasibility. Participation information indicates openness to this format, but neither staffing nor equipment access is an agreed commitment.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a first experiment on replicated, production-welded painted crossmember-to-stringer joints. AutomVoj would supply specimens and service records; Erić would develop testing and fracture analysis. Compare room-temperature workshop loading with an eight-cell chilled factorial: baseline, thermal cycling alone, condensation alone, vibration alone, each pair, and all three. Hold geometry, weld condition, mean load and mean chilled temperature comparable; independently define factor levels from measured service histories, including 2–6 °C operation. Measure cycles to a consistently defined crack-initiation endpoint, crack growth, location and fracture morphology. Proposed go/no-go: proceed to mitigation only if independent confirmation reproduces the site crack location and morphology and an explanatory contrast has a confidence interval excluding zero; otherwise extend investigation. Set replication from pilot variability and do not pass insufficient evidence. Null single-factor effects do not exclude interactions. Judge the best-supported explanation, not a preferred combination; accelerated results cannot establish 18-month durability.
- Propose an initial two-week feasibility and evidence phase, followed by an eight-week pilot after equipment reservation, both negotiable. Review photographs and both retained sections before agreeing destructive sampling; collect steel, welding, filler, coating and inspection records plus site strain, vibration, temperature and moisture histories. Reserve company engineering time and verify chamber size, condensation control and safe combined loading before testing.
- Use separate pilot/tuning and held-out confirmation specimens, checking production-batch and service-history independence. Validate transfer on actual painted connections at 2–6 °C, not pooled colder-temperature specimens. If confirmation material is unavailable, retain an unresolved prerequisite. Following a supported mechanism, propose separately validated welding, filler or joint-design changes; use fracture-mechanics modelling only after checking its assumptions against measured damage.
