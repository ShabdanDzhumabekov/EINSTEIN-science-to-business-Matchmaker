# Locating and controlling manganese release in AquaCikl greywater recycling

## Verified facts
- AquaCikl's analysers read 0.18 mg/L manganese at the coagulation outlet across three client sites; SR-IED-2023 limits are 0.10 mg/L manganese and 0.30 mg/L iron. [F1]
- One large laundry client faces reinspection in five months. [F2]
- Raw greywater entering the plant contains 0.04-0.07 mg/L manganese, and the company can pull samples at every process stage with its on-site kit. [F3]
- Increasing coagulant dose by 30% and dropping pH from 7.4 to 6.9 during coagulation gave no meaningful improvement. [F4]
- The company's acceptable outcome is a root-cause finding and a specific process change implementable at existing installations without replacing major equipment. [F5]
- The company can dedicate two senior process engineers, one lab technician, up to four months, and access to three operating installations. [F6]
- Prof. Žigić's group focuses on transition-metal speciation, including iron and manganese, and redox transformations in aqueous systems. [F7]
- Prof. Žigić prefers collaborations where the Faculty of Technology laboratory performs analytical work rather than acting solely as a subcontractor. [F8]

## Our inference
- The raw-to-outlet gap and failed dose/pH trials suggest manganese is released or held in filters/tanks, so coagulation-only optimisation likely misses the source; stage-wise speciation is needed.
- Her manganese/iron work likely transfers conceptually, but published manganese coagulation evidence is synthetic water with higher Mn and alum/KMnO4, not PACl/ferric laundry greywater, so transfer must be tested.
- Her laboratory capability fits a joint project, but availability is unknown, making timing conditional against the five-month deadline.

## Proposed next steps
- Proposed first experiment: at one affected laundry site, sample raw greywater, post-coagulation, post-filter and holding-tank outlet daily for four weeks, plus filter-media and tank-sediment cores. Baseline: current PACl/ferric operation. Proposed company contribution: process flow, site access and on-site kit readings; proposed researcher contribution: ICP-OES and voltammetric/spectrophotometric manganese speciation plus jar tests on real greywater. Run stage-isolated tests changing one factor per series: current versus prior dose/pH, filtered versus unfiltered, and tank-sediment contact. Measure dissolved, colloidal and particulate Mn, Fe, turbidity, pH and ORP. Go/no-go by month 3: identify one stage where Mn rises above the 0.04-0.07 mg/L raw range and a candidate adjustment keeping outlet Mn <=0.10 mg/L and Fe <=0.30 mg/L in bench/pilot tests without major equipment replacement; otherwise expand diagnosis.
- Proposed timeline: weeks 1-2 sampling plan; weeks 3-8 stage sampling and speciation; weeks 9-14 jar/column tests isolating coagulation, filter media and tank cycling; weeks 15-16 report and options, targeting month 4 before the five-month reinspection.
- Proposed success criteria, as targets not guarantees: one site verifies outlet Mn <=0.10 mg/L and Fe <=0.30 mg/L over two weeks under existing operation after one stage-specific change; two independent sample sets link the increase to a named stage; the change uses existing equipment; reinspection package ready before deadline.
- Proposed second phase if filter media or tank cycling is implicated: column tests with current media and tank sediment under real greywater, one factor per run, Faculty lab analysis and company field support; stop if no stage-specific cause or major equipment replacement is required.
