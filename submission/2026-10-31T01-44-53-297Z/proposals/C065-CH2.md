# Hybrid Recommender Prototype for Small E-Commerce Catalogues

## Verified facts
- The company's material states that with only 500–2,000 transactions per client its collaborative filtering models gave worse results than simple best-seller sorting, with the largest dataset at 1,900 completed orders over eighteen months and the smallest around 520, and that it cannot wait for larger datasets; it also reports the purchase co-occurrence matrix was too sparse to be useful. [F1]
- The company's material reports that simple best-seller sorting outperformed the model in its one client A/B test and that a working prototype for one client would be enough to start. [F2]
- The company can give full read access to anonymised transaction and product catalogue data under a data-sharing agreement. [F3]
- The company can allocate one mid-level developer for roughly one day per week and reports it has not done a structured collaboration with a university or research institution, while Erić is cautious about commitments requiring more than one day per week outside the faculty during the academic semester. [F4]
- The client stack includes PHP/Laravel backend development, MySQL/MariaDB transactional data, and WooCommerce with custom REST API integrations. [F5]
- Erić lists joint projects with retail technology companies as open; her dissertation concerned hybrid recommendation strategies for data-sparse retail environments, and her group combines content-based filtering with behavioural signals from sparse transaction logs typical of small and medium retail businesses. [F6]
- Erić's work shows structured product attribute data can be encoded into feature vectors for new-item recommendation, but it notes limitations in attribute completeness and supplier data availability, and warns that content-based methods tend to over-specialise recommendations. [F7]
- Erić's matrix-factorization study found hit rate drops below the non-personalised popularity baseline at 0.3% density. [F8]

## Our inference
- Because Erić's evaluations use grocery, clothing, pharmacy and home-goods retail data rather than Harmat's e-commerce catalogues, transfer to a Harmat client is plausible but unproven; the first test should therefore be a one-client transfer check.
- A content-heavy hybrid is more likely to beat best-seller sorting on small catalogues if metadata is rich enough, but sparse co-occurrence and over-specialisation could erase the gain, so a one-factor-at-a-time comparison should show which signal matters.
- The proposal is conditional because Erić's availability is unknown and semester-limited; joint-project willingness is open, but a one-day-per-week cadence still needs her confirmation.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment: select one Harmat client after a data-sharing agreement and use its anonymised completed orders, expected in the 500–2,000 range, plus product catalogue metadata, split by time into train/test; baseline is best-seller sorting. Build two variants: content-only attribute similarity and a content plus session/co-occurrence hybrid. Measure offline precision@5 and recall@5 against the baseline, then, if offline results justify it, run a small A/B test on product pages against best-seller. Researcher contribution: Erić designs and evaluates the recommender; company contribution: Harmat supplies data access and one developer for Laravel/MySQL/WooCommerce integration and logging. This tests transfer from Erić's published retail settings to one Harmat catalogue. Go/no-go: proceed only if the hybrid beats best-seller by an agreed relative margin (proposed ≥10% on precision@5) and metadata completeness passes an agreed threshold; stop if metadata is too incomplete or no variant beats popularity. Proposed timeline: 4 weeks data audit and offline test, 4–6 weeks integration/A/B if go.
- Agree before start the A/B success criterion: metric (click-through or add-to-cart rate), minimum relative lift over best-seller, evaluation window and minimum traffic; treat these as negotiation targets, not guarantees, and confirm the one-client test has enough traffic for reliability.
- Audit product metadata per client: descriptions, categories, attributes, price tier, supplier and languages; quantify completeness and flag fields needed for attribute vectors and explanations.
- Run a one-factor-at-a-time comparison (best-seller, content-only, content+session, content+co-occurrence) on the same temporal split to identify which factor drives any gain, and document integration constraints for Laravel/MySQL/WooCommerce.
