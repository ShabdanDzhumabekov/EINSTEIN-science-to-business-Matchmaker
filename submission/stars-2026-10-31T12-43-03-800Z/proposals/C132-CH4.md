# Joint project: reliable cross-script Serbian contract search

## Verified facts
- The company estimates that roughly one in five searches was affected by cross-script misses in a January 2024 server-log sample. It attributes the problem to its PostgreSQL full-text index treating Cyrillic and Latin as different character sets. [F1]
- The company's brief identifies mixed-script documents, dialect-dependent Latin-to-Cyrillic ambiguity and response-time sensitivity as concerns. [F2]
- The company reports one backend engineer available for up to six weeks. Dragić's notes describe two active projects and capacity for one small industrial contract or consultancy. [F3]
- Dragić states a preference for clearly defined, sufficiently large Serbian-language corpora and research-oriented collaboration rather than general software development. [F4]
- The company's technology inventory lists PostgreSQL full-text search, Docker and Kubernetes in a Serbian private cloud, and a self-assessed ISO 27001-aligned internal security policy. [F5]
- Dragić's work evaluates phoneme-based canonicalization on court decisions. His separate latency study reports a tradeoff: transformer normalization improved retrieval quality but added 47 ms at the 99th percentile. [F6]
- Dragić lists openness to joint projects with companies managing large Serbian-language document collections. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could identify a practical normalization placement and quantify remaining missed matches, relevance losses and latency costs before deployment.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permitted access, Dragić could test how canonicalization placement transfers from prior retrieval settings to mixed-script contracts in PostgreSQL, using company-specific relevance judgments and workload measurements.

## Our inference
- This suggests a focused joint project could resolve normalization placement rather than merely implement transliteration. Court-decision evidence does not validate contract retrieval, PostgreSQL compatibility or latency under this company's workload.
- Canonicalization could conflate distinct legal terms; ambiguous digraphs, names and identifiers could remain failure sources. Neural fallback could add latency, while new terminology would likely require exception maintenance.
- The proposal is conditional: joint-project openness is stated, but Dragić's start-date availability is unknown and his capacity is limited. The historical failure estimate may not describe current traffic or isolate script mismatch from parsing and morphology.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a PostgreSQL sandbox experiment using company contracts and representative queries, with separate tuning and held-out evaluation samples whose independence is checked by grouping duplicate queries and related contracts. Compare unchanged search, index-only normalization, query-only normalization and both, holding parsing, morphology and ranking fixed. Propose Dragić designs canonicalization and exception tests; the company engineer integrates and replays representative load, with legal reviewers judging every evaluation query and candidate result. Include both cross-script directions, mixed-script documents, ambiguous mappings, names and identifiers. Select the best configuration on tuning data. Proposed go/no-go on held-out data: halve the zero-hit rate among queries with reviewer-confirmed existing relevant contracts, lose at most one percentage point in P@10, and add at most 5% to p95/p99 latency under matched load. Count each query execution once, without merging; use all confirmed-answerable executions as the failure-rate denominator. Require these criteria separately for mixed-script documents and cross-script directions. Unresolved cases count conservatively against acceptance; insufficient confidence or missing population data means no pass.
- Propose week 1 for corpus sizing, access approval, current-failure reproduction and threshold negotiation; weeks 2–4 for the controlled comparison and tuning; weeks 5–6 for frozen evaluation and integration review. Agree sample sizes and confidence procedures before testing. Audit parsing failures separately, establish PostgreSQL tokenizer/index compatibility, and retain the placement comparison even if one configuration shows no benefit.
- Keep experiments within approved private-cloud and security arrangements. Before proceeding, agree corpus sufficiency, reviewer capacity, tool licensing, budget, researcher hours, reindexing/storage limits and rollback. Measure actual integration effort and resource use; feasibility within the engineer's six-week allocation is an additional go/no-go gate. These are proposed procedures and targets, not agreed commitments.
