# Morphology- and Script-Aware Serbian Support Search

## Verified facts
- In a sample of 12,000 real support queries from one bank client about 18% returned no useful result; more than half of those misses were traced to morphological mismatch or script confusion, and the stated outcome is getting that 18% miss rate below 5% and keeping it there with a manageable maintenance process. [F1]
- The work must be testable within six months and fit a modest tooling/data budget of around 8,000 EUR. [F2]
- LinguaBot holds about 85,000 labelled query-response pairs across all clients, and two in-house linguists can annotate or validate proposed solutions. [F3]
- Users freely switch between Latin and Cyrillic script mid-sentence; the current Elasticsearch setup uses a Croatian-built basic stemmer, and the stack includes spaCy with custom Serbian morphological components. [F4]
- The indexing approach must be updatable when new vocabulary arrives without a full re-index. [F5]
- Dragić's group works on mixed-script Serbian information retrieval, script normalization, transliteration and inverted indexes robust to script variation, and has studied how stemming and lemmatisation interact with script normalization during index construction. [F6]
- His phoneme-based canonicalization reduced the out-of-vocabulary rate from 9.3% to 2.1% against a Latin-only baseline on a legal gold standard, and his real-time retrieval work addresses incremental index updating under low latency. [F7]
- He has an open-source script-normalization module compatible with popular search frameworks, prior contract research adapting a search module for Serbian court decisions with a Novi Sad startup, and is open to contract research on internal search engine customization. [F8]
- His notes state two active projects but capacity for one smaller industrial contract or consultancy; his exact availability for this format is not confirmed. [F9]

## Our inference
- The strongest published evidence comes from news, legal, web and benchmark corpora rather than bank support chat, so transfer to LinguaBot's 12,000-query sample and 85,000 labelled pairs is an open test rather than an established result.
- Because fine-tuned multilingual models still underperform on within-sentence script mixing, residual misses likely remain; a cascaded deterministic-plus-neural-fallback normalizer is a plausible candidate to test.
- Since manual synonym lists do not scale, an incrementally updated exception and lemma resource with a named owner likely matters as much as the initial accuracy gain.
- A six-month, ~8,000 EUR frame suggests a focused pilot on one product and one index rather than production hardening across all clients.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment (months 1–2): freeze a held-out slice of LinguaBot's 12,000 bank queries and 85,000 labelled pairs, fix a written definition of 'no useful result', and measure the current Elasticsearch/Croatian-stemmer baseline. Then test deterministic Latin/Cyrillic normalization, phoneme canonicalization and morphology-aware lemma indexing inside the existing Elasticsearch/spaCy stack. Researcher proposes the normalization, canonicalization and index design; company supplies data, environment and linguist validation time. Proposed go/no-go: continue only if the held-out miss rate falls materially toward the below-5% target and residual errors are analysable by category (morphology, script mixing, vocabulary).
- Months 3–4: test incremental vocabulary update by holding out quarter-specific product terms; measure whether adding them avoids a full re-index and how much linguist time it costs.
- Months 5–6: validate on independent labelled pairs with the in-house linguists; measure miss rate, the script-mixing failure subset and latency against baseline, then decide on a production pilot.
- Negotiation targets, not guarantees: held-out miss rate below 5%; vocabulary updates without full re-index; agreed latency overhead versus the current baseline; documented maintenance owner.
