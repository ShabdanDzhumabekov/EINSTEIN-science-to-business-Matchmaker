# Cold-store conveyor weld cracking: thermo-mechanical fatigue diagnosis and joint fix

## Verified facts
- AutomVoj's conveyor frames are MIG/MAG-welded from mild steel hollow section and painted before delivery. [F1]
- Site cracking is reported at crossmember-to-stringer weld joints after about 18 months in cold stores typically at 2 °C to 6 °C; AutomVoj cannot reproduce it at room temperature and lacks low-temperature cyclic test equipment/know-how. [F2]
- AutomVoj has photographs, site measurements and two failed sections in storage for examination. [F3]
- Erić's climate chamber and servo-hydraulic machine are shared with two other groups, so extended campaigns need advance coordination. [F4]
- AutomVoj has no dedicated R&D department; development is led by the chief engineer and two senior automation engineers alongside live projects. [F5]
- AutomVoj wants an actionable change to welding procedure, filler material or joint design to stop warranty replacements. [F6]
- Erić's group has servo-hydraulic fatigue machines, a −60 °C climate chamber and Abaqus FE models. [F7]
- Erić has reported that superimposed thermal cycling during mechanical fatigue alters crack initiation and growth in butt-welded S275JR plates. [F8]
- Erić has reported that PWHT increased predicted endurance at 90 MPa from 1.2 × 10⁶ to 3.8 × 10⁶ cycles. [F9]

## Our inference
- The fit is plausible because the suspected mechanism—loaded-belt vibration plus cold-store thermal cycling/condensation—is close to Erić's thermo-mechanical fatigue work; however his low-temperature evidence is mostly −20 °C to −60 °C, so 2–6 °C condensation-assisted fatigue is a transfer gap to test, not an established match.
- Specimen-to-frame scale is another gap: coupon/cruciform results must reproduce the site weld-toe failure mode before application to full frames; with no stated site load/temperature record, the profile is an assumption. Shared equipment and no R&D department argue for a short go/no-go campaign.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment: company supplies WPS, filler grades, dimensions and the two failed sections; researcher performs fractography and builds sub-component crossmember-to-stringer fillet-welded mild-steel hollow-section specimens to production WPS. Baseline: room-temperature loaded-belt vibration spectrum. Changed condition: 2–6 °C with condensation and jointly defined thermal cycles plus same mechanical spectrum. Measure crack initiation (5% stiffness drop or dye-penetrant indication), growth, residual stress and failure location. Go/no-go: continue only if cold-wet condition shows at least 30% shorter crack-initiation life (proposed threshold) or site-like weld-toe cracking.
- If go, test one or two variants (PWHT, alternative filler or joint detail) against the reproduced baseline; proposed success criterion is at least 2× measured crack-initiation life or removal of the site-like cracking mode. These are negotiation targets, not guarantees.
- Researcher contributes climate-chamber/servo-hydraulic testing and FE crack-driving analysis; company contributes specimens, production data, site access and engineering review. Proposed timeline: weeks 1–4 data/section review and specimen design; weeks 5–10 baseline and cold-wet first campaign; weeks 11–12 go/no-go and variant selection; months 4–6 variant testing/analysis. Timeline is conditional because Erić's availability is unknown and equipment access is shared.
