# Per-site automaton baselines for unlabelled Modbus/BACnet drift detection

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec has no confirmed malicious captures and no labelled attack data; supervised classifiers trained on public ICS data produced many false positives in real deployments and clients switched alerts off. The required method must work with no attack examples and every alert must be explainable to a plant manager in plain language. [F1]
- The detector must learn what normal looks like on a specific site and raise a flag when behaviour drifts from that site-specific baseline. [F2]
- Training data are several terabytes of normal captures from consenting clients over three years; the company can dedicate one to two engineers part-time and has one engineer who has self-studied anomaly detection for six months, with traffic archives, hardware bench and firmware codebase under NDA. [F3]
- ProtoSense passively inspects Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP; a planned fifth hardware variant in late 2025 is to have a dedicated security processor for on-device anomaly detection without affecting packet forwarding latency. [F4]
- Hadžić's published method infers an automaton from normal traffic as a baseline and flags messages causing transitions absent from the model; on a SCADA test-environment dataset with injected anomalies it reported precision 0.91 and recall 0.87 averaged across anomaly types. She is open to joint company projects on protocol analysis or network security, offers GInferLib (an open-source Python library with seven grammatical-inference algorithms), and contributed inference algorithms and evaluation methodology to a SCADA anomaly-detection pilot with BEZ Systems (2018–2020). [F5]

## Our inference
- Published precision and recall are on one SCADA protocol in a test environment with injected anomalies, not real Modbus/BACnet traffic; they cannot be assumed and need a transfer test.
- No labelled attacks means evaluation likely needs controlled bench injection; real incidents or maintenance windows could only be weak labels.
- Field mutation or register tampering may not change message sequences, and unseen normal states may be flagged; this could reproduce false-positive fatigue.
- Automaton transition explanations may not be understandable to plant managers; that is untested.
- This proposal is conditional: Hadžić's availability is unknown and she has not confirmed a time commitment; her recorded openness is to joint projects, not to a schedule.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment: from NDA-cleared captures at one Modbus TCP and one BACnet/IP site, split normal traffic into training and holdout and infer per-site DFAs with GInferLib/RPNI (researcher). Baseline: current ProtoSense alert export if available, otherwise a rule-based sequence checker. On the hardware-in-the-loop bench with physical PLC simulators, inject replay, truncation, out-of-order, field mutation and register tampering.
- Measure on holdout: false alerts per site-week, precision/recall by anomaly type, and explanation time. Go/no-go: proceed only if held-out false alerts are within the company-tolerated rate and sequence-changing anomalies reach proposed recall ≥0.80; field mutation/register tampering is reported separately and not a pass criterion unless detected.
- Transfer/coverage test: compare bench-trained with site-trained models and review holdout traces for unseen normal transitions before flagging. This tests the gap from test-environment SCADA data to real Modbus/BACnet.
- Explainability and integration: generate a plain-language alert from each flagged transition and validate with a plant manager (proposed target ≥80% correctly explained); profile CPU, memory and latency on current and planned hardware rather than inferring hardware fit from model size.
- Proposed 12-week timeline: weeks 1–2 data/NDA and baseline export; 3–6 inference and holdout; 7–9 bench injection; 10–11 explanation and hardware profiling; week 12 go/no-go. These success targets and dates are negotiation hypotheses, not agreed budgets, guaranteed performance or achieved outcomes.
