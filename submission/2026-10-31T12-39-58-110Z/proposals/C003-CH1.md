# Investigating chilled-service cracking in AutomVoj conveyor welds

## Verified facts
- AutomVoj describes painted mild-steel hollow-section frames, with cracking at crossmember-to-stringer welds after about 18 months of continuous use. It reports replacing 14 complete assemblies under warranty this year. [F1]
- The company reports cold-store operation at 2–6 °C, while its loaded room-temperature workshop tests pass. It suspects thermal cycling, condensation and loaded-belt vibration acting together; this is not an established cause. [F2]
- AutomVoj has site photographs, crack measurements and two retained failed sections, rather than a documented representative sample of all warranty failures. [F3]
- AutomVoj lacks equipment and know-how for low-temperature cyclic-load testing. Erić's climate chamber and servo-hydraulic machine are shared with two other research groups. [F4]
- The company has no dedicated R&D department; its chief engineer and two senior automation engineers divide their time between live projects and improvement work. [F5]
- Erić's thermal–mechanical fatigue study tested butt-welded S275JR plates between −30 °C and +20 °C. Out-of-phase loading produced shorter crack-initiation life than the room-temperature baseline. [F6]
- Erić lists openness to joint projects involving fatigue testing under low-temperature or combined loading conditions. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could distinguish individual and interacting contributors to cracking and show whether a practical joint modification merits longer service validation.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement, Erić could test whether thermal–mechanical fatigue methods transfer to painted conveyor joints in mildly chilled, moisture-exposed service, using failure evidence and representative specimens.

## Our inference
- This suggests a joint project could connect service measurements, fracture examination and controlled fatigue comparisons, rather than assume that cold temperature alone causes cracking.
- Transfer remains unvalidated: the cited plate study differs in material, geometry, coating and temperature. Its shorter-life result highlights a damage risk, not a prediction for AutomVoj; accelerated testing could also reproduce the wrong mechanism.
- The proposal is conditional because Erić's availability is unknown, despite his stated openness to this format. Shared equipment and limited company engineering capacity likely make a staged campaign more practical.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose testing representative painted hollow-section crossmember joints, informed by examination of the retained failures. Use a full eight-cell matrix: baseline, T, M, V, TM, TV, MV and TMV. Define T as steady chilled temperature versus measured thermal cycling; M as dry versus controlled condensation; V as absent versus measured belt vibration. Independently control these factors, retaining identical base mechanical loading, fabrication and coating, and add a matched room-temperature loaded comparator. Measure cycles to a predefined detectable crack, growth rate, origin and fracture morphology. Proposed go/no-go: replicated cracking must match the observed connection, origin and morphology under representative 2–6 °C service exposure; isolated-factor null results do not close the investigation. Select any modification using tuning specimens, then test the best configuration on separate held-out chilled-service specimens. A proposed improvement gate is at least 25% longer crack-initiation life than the current joint, with uncertainty excluding no improvement. Insufficient evidence means no pass; neither reproduction nor accelerated improvement establishes elimination of 18-month warranty failures.
- Subject to agreement, AutomVoj would supply fabrication records, representative joints and site-measurement support; Erić would lead examination, experimental design and fatigue analysis. Reserve company engineering time and shared laboratory slots, and agree destructive allocation of the two sections before cutting. Propose a four-week feasibility stage followed by an eight-week pilot, conditional on bookings and measurable crack development rather than an agreed completion promise.
- Verify fixture, humidity-control and corrosion-analysis capability before testing, seeking additional expertise if needed. Record actual loads, thermal transients and moisture exposure before setting factor levels. Allocate separate tuning and evaluation specimens and check their independence across fabrication batches. Compare welding, filler or geometry changes only after mechanism evidence emerges; retain all pairwise interactions in the matrix and use follow-up tests where results remain ambiguous.
