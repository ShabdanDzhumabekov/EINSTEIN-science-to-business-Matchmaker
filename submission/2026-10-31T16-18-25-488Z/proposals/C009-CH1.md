# Serbian morphology and mixed-script retrieval for LinguaBot

## Verified facts
- LinguaBot reports that, in a sample of 12,000 real support queries from one bank client, about 18% returned no useful result. Its analysis attributed more than half of those misses to morphological mismatch or script confusion. [F1]
- The company describes an Elasticsearch baseline using a Croatian-oriented stemmer that misses Serbian-specific forms, and users switching scripts even within words. [F2]
- LinguaBot reports that manual synonym-list maintenance does not scale as clients add product terminology every quarter. [F3]
- The company has around 85,000 labelled query-response pairs across all clients and two in-house linguists who can annotate or validate proposed solutions. [F4]
- The company's stated tooling or data-work budget is around EUR 8,000. [F5]
- Nikolić’s lemmatizer uses a unified internal representation before lemmatization and restores the original script afterward. Her tokenization work normalizes script boundaries within tokens. Her retrieval study reports the highest F1 for full lemmatization combined with paradigm-aware indexing. [F6]
- Her morphological lexicon study reports 91.3% paradigm correctness for common vocabulary and 78.6% for borrowings. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could establish whether Serbian-aware retrieval meets the miss-rate target under vocabulary updates, while exposing relevance losses and maintenance costs before deployment.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and approved access, Nikolić could test how corpus-derived morphology transfers to banking and telecom support language, using validated query judgements and terminology-update cases.

## Our inference
- This suggests a strong methodological match, but reference-corpus and social-media results do not establish banking or telecom retrieval performance. Borrowing errors are a transfer risk; normalization or expansion could also merge distinct terms and conceal relevance losses.
- Fixing morphology and script handling likely addresses an important subset of misses, but the reported attribution does not establish that these changes alone can reach below 5%.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional on availability. Nikolić is listed as open to joint projects, but her availability is unknown; staffing and a six-month commitment remain unconfirmed.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose diagnosing the 12,000-query sample and separating tuning/evaluation samples from approximately 85,000 pairs; verify client, time and duplicate-family independence. Compare Elasticsearch with independently defined lemmatization, within-token normalization and paradigm-aware retrieval: baseline, each alone, all pairs and all three, holding documents and ranking controls comparable. Propose Nikolić lead linguistic design and LinguaBot implement and validate relevance. Select the best configuration on tuning data; evaluate once on held-out original-bank and broader-client queries separately, including inflection, mixed-script and unseen-term slices. Proposed go/no-go requires upper 95% miss-rate bounds below 5% in both populations, precision acceptance, automatic inflection/script handling, and simulated quarterly updates preserving these results without full re-indexing. Apply statistical gates before and after replay. Missing independent original-bank data or insufficient evidence means no pass. Also require agreed maintenance/latency limits, tooling/data spend within approximately EUR 8,000 and a testable implementation within six months.
- Review every evaluation query; count each occurrence once without deduplication. Miss rate is queries without useful results divided by all evaluated queries; unresolved judgements count as misses. Propose precision as mean relevant fraction at an agreed cutoff, unresolved results counted irrelevant. Agree a precision non-inferiority margin and paired confidence procedure beforehand. Require the lower 95% bound on candidate-minus-baseline precision to exceed the negative margin in both populations before and after replay; record annotation disagreement.
- Propose months one–two for permissions, definitions and baseline reproduction; three–four for comparisons; five–six for locked evaluation and replay. Prototype query-time expansion or versioned dictionaries against actual mappings. Record update duration, staff minutes, corrected paradigms, affected documents, latency and tooling/data expenditure; agree numeric maintenance/latency limits before evaluation. Test newly introduced terms and repeated updates for transfer and sustainability, rather than assuming corpus accuracy establishes deployment suitability.

## Open questions
- Can Nikolić confirm availability for this joint project, and can both parties identify implementation, annotation and maintenance owners and funding for their time?
- Can client permissions, de-identification and secure access support the proposed samples, including independent held-out queries from the original bank?
- What defines a useful result, retrieval cutoff, precision non-inferiority margin and latency/maintenance limits, and does the below-5% target extend to every client separately?
- Which Elasticsearch mappings and update operations are available, what counts as a full re-index, and do tool licences, compute capacity and deployment restrictions permit the proposed implementation?
