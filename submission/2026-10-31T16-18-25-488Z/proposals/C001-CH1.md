# Explainable, Site-Specific Anomaly Detection for ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec reports several terabytes of consenting-client captures collected over three years, no labelled malicious traffic, and the possibility of previously undetected compromise. [F1]
- The company describes ProtoSense as passive inspection for Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP. [F2]
- ArmaturaSec reports on-device inference hardware and custom Yocto Linux. Its hardware-development plan discusses anomaly detection and forwarding latency together; numeric resource limits are not supplied. [F3]
- The company reports that excessive false positives caused clients to disable alerts and identifies plant managers as the audience for explanations. [F4]
- ArmaturaSec describes capacity of one to two part-time engineers and archive, bench and firmware access under NDA. [F5]
- Kiss's published Modbus work constructs per-register statistical baselines and documents seasonal baseline drift. [F6]
- Kiss lists joint projects with ICS-monitoring companies among her interests. Her group's testbed access is described under a data-sharing agreement. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The pilot could establish whether useful, understandable alerts are feasible without labelled attack training data, while exposing BACnet, alert-burden or hardware barriers before deployment.
- For the researcher: Conditional on agreement and permissions, Kiss could test cross-protocol calibration and drift handling using operational captures and controlled bench cases.

## Our inference
- Site-specific statistical baselines suggest a plausible alternative to supervised attack classification, explaining deviations in timing, values or commands. Seasonal variation could nevertheless recreate alert fatigue.
- Modbus evidence does not establish BACnet effectiveness or ProtoSense hardware fit. Contaminated archives and aggressive adaptation could conceal attacks; controlled bench detection cannot establish coverage of unknown real attacks.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional: Kiss is open to this format, but her availability is unknown. Client collection consent also does not establish permission for research sharing.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a six-week pilot using representative per-site Modbus and BACnet archives as unlabelled data, plus safe bench injections. Separate training, tuning and held-out evaluation periods; verify independence and operating-cycle coverage. Compare fixed-threshold rules with statistical one-class baselines; select the best configuration using tuning data without attack-labelled training or threshold selection. Each protocol must separately pass held-out operational and bench evaluation. Proposed gates: a lower 95% confidence bound of at least 80% injected-episode detection within 60 seconds; an upper 95% confidence bound of at most one false-alert episode per observed site-day; agreed device-budget compliance; and no measurable forwarding-latency increase under matched load and agreed measurement tolerances. Require plant managers to correctly explain four of five randomly sampled alerts under a pre-agreed rubric. Missing data, insufficient precision or any failed gate means no deployment approval. Propose company engineers prepare permitted captures and Yocto integration, with Kiss's group developing features and calibration; use weeks one–two for preparation, three–four for tuning and five–six for locked evaluation.
- Propose merging same-site, device and deviation-type alerts separated by at most five minutes into episodes. Review every evaluation episode; count unresolved episodes as false alerts. Divide by observed monitoring hours/24; pre-agree site-day-cluster-aware confidence bounds. Independently label a uniform random sample of all non-overlapping evaluation windows, alerted or not, recording inclusion probabilities. Use these labels to audit contamination, not estimate episode totals; measure detection only on injected or confirmed cases. Insufficient exposure or precision prevents acceptance.
- Before tuning, agree and freeze protocol-specific challenge matrices, episode boundaries, repetition allocation and detection-confidence calculations; keep evaluation outcomes unavailable for configuration selection. Include Modbus replay, coil writes and scans; BACnet unauthorised property writes, discovery sweeps and flooding; and stable/high-load operation, maintenance and reconfiguration controls. Test BACnet features separately and compare frozen versus operator-approved baseline updates against legitimate changes and persistent anomalies. Allocate repetitions across families and conditions so repeated easy cases cannot determine acceptance.

## Open questions
- Can Kiss confirm availability for this joint project, and can both parties agree the proposed responsibilities, part-time engineering scope, budget and six-week schedule?
- Which appliance is current, was the security-processor variant released, and what CPU, memory, storage, throughput and latency budgets and measurement tolerances should govern matched-load testing?
- Can representative held-out captures for both protocols, operating logs and client research-sharing permissions be secured under suitable NDA, testbed data-sharing, retention and firmware terms?
- Who will adjudicate episodes and approve baseline changes, and can operators accept the proposed burden, delay and review workload, with five alerts randomly sampled per protocol and correctness defined beforehand as identifying the affected device, observed deviation and reason for concern in plain language?
