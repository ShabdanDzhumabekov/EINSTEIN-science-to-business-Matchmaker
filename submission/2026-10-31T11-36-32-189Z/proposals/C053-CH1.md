# Tracing manganese increases and restoring iron–manganese compliance

## Verified facts
- AquaCikl reports that SR-IED-2023 lowered treated-discharge limits to 0.10 mg/L manganese and 0.30 mg/L iron. [F1]
- The company reports inlet manganese of 0.04–0.07 mg/L versus 0.18 mg/L at the coagulation outlet across three client sites. Increasing coagulant dose by 30 percent and lowering coagulation pH from 7.4 to 6.9 brought no meaningful improvement. [F2]
- AquaCikl seeks a root-cause finding and a specific change implementable at existing installations without major equipment replacement. One large laundry client faces reinspection in five months. [F3]
- The company can dedicate two senior process engineers and one lab technician for up to four months, with access to three operating installations for field testing. [F4]
- Žigić prefers collaborations in which the Faculty laboratory performs analytical work rather than acting solely as a subcontractor. The department’s ICP-OES is available for contract analysis through the university service-centre agreement. [F5]
- Žigić’s manganese jar-test study used synthetic water containing 0.5–4.0 mg/L dissolved Mn(II). It reported conversion of Mn(II) to colloidal MnO₂ using KMnO₄ pre-oxidation before coagulation. [F6]
- Her continuous-flow iron–manganese study reported that, at a 1:1 Fe/Mn ratio, competition for coagulant capacity reduced iron removal by 12 percentage points. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could distinguish genuine manganese addition or release from sampling and hydraulic effects, and identify whether an equipment-light operational change can meet both reported limits before reinspection.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and sample access, Žigić could test how real industrial greywater alters metal release, redox cycling and coupled removal, using stage samples and controlled treatment comparisons.

## Our inference
- This joint project is conditional on Žigić’s availability and reserved laboratory capacity, which remain unknown. Instrument availability alone does not establish analytical turnaround capacity.
- The published oxidation–coagulation work suggests a useful experimental approach, not a validated greywater solution. Transfer from higher-concentration synthetic water requires real-matrix validation; the reported iron-removal tradeoff makes checking both metals essential.
- The concentration discrepancy suggests investigating metal inputs, release and recycling alongside incomplete removal. Unmatched residence times, flows or analytical fractions could also explain an apparent increase; media release and tank cycling should remain hypotheses rather than assumed causes.

## Proposed next steps
- First, propose residence-time- and flow-matched stage sampling across all three installations, including recycle streams, actual greywater, reagents and fresh/spent media. Measure total/dissolved Mn and Fe, pH, dissolved oxygen and metal mass flows, using blanks, spikes and external confirmation. Use separate tuning and evaluation batches; check their independence. Against current-operation controls, run an explicit diagnostic matrix: unchanged reference; media contact only; tank redox/residence change only; reagent-batch substitution only; combined changes. Hold other factors fixed. Judge reproduction against the observed inlet-to-coagulation-outlet manganese increase, not an assumed mechanism; null individual effects narrow hypotheses without ending investigation. Then compare feasible oxidation/coagulation adjustments on real greywater and field-verify the best configuration. Proposed go/no-go requires evidence explaining the observed increase and an implementable change achieving Mn ≤0.10 and Fe ≤0.30 mg/L under the confirmed discharge sampling protocol, without major equipment replacement, within four months and before reinspection. Otherwise, do not claim readiness; report unresolved causes and feasibility barriers.
- Negotiate a four-month programme: month one for hydraulic reconciliation and matrix-validation checks, month two for controlled diagnosis, month three for treatment tuning, and month four for independent field verification and an operating recommendation. Confirm legal limits, sampling location and total-versus-dissolved definitions before acceptance testing; agree evaluation frequency and uncertainty handling before collecting verification samples.
- Propose that AquaCikl’s engineers lead hydraulic mapping and safe operational trials, with its technician coordinating sampling. Subject to agreement, Žigić would lead experimental design and Faculty-led analytical interpretation. Arrange service-centre access, turnaround, external checks and costs before starting; nominate an operator to maintain and verify any selected change after the staffing window.

## Open questions
- Can Žigić confirm availability for this joint project and can the Faculty reserve suitable analytical capacity throughout the proposed four-month programme?
- What budget and permitted operational changes can support sampling, Faculty analyses, external confirmation and safe field trials without compromising production or reuse quality?
- What legally applicable discharge sampling protocol, analytical definitions and current iron measurements should determine acceptance?
- Can AquaCikl supply hydraulic diagrams, flow and residence-time records, recycle histories, original analytical procedures, and representative reagent, media and tank-deposit samples?
