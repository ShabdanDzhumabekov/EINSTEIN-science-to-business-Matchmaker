# Joint project: a tested post-quantum signing path for KL-HSM

## Verified facts
- Kriptolog states that KL-HSM has limited microcontroller flash and RAM, making a straight drop-in replacement impossible. Its cryptographic firmware uses ARM Cortex-M and C. [F1]
- The company has not adapted its architecture for larger keys or changed signature flows. It wants a migration plan identifying supporting firmware versions, performance trade-offs and hybrid classical/post-quantum operation. [F2]
- Kriptolog defines a good outcome as a documented roadmap and at least one working integration proof of concept within twelve months. It describes modest collaborative capacity, conditional on well-defined, practically applicable work. [F3]
- The company's technology list includes PKCS#11, RFC 5280 and RFC 6960, and Common Criteria evaluation methodology targeting EAL4+. [F4]
- Nikolić prefers real-hardware collaboration with measurable deliverables. Her laboratory has only two Infineon SLE97 evaluation kits; her notes say additional partner hardware would accelerate shared projects. [F5]
- Nikolić's work implements finalist-era Dilithium and Falcon in C. Her separate Dilithium study reports timing-leakage detection in three of eight compiler/architecture combinations, highlighting an implementation-security risk rather than an assurance of constant-time behaviour. [F6]
- Her hybrid-HSM prototype combines classical ECDH on an SLE97 secure microcontroller with Kyber-768 encapsulation on a separate FPGA; it concerns key exchange, not hybrid signing. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could replace an open-ended evaluation with measured feasibility evidence and a defensible versioned migration plan, including explicit hardware, interoperability and security limitations.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and access, Nikolić could investigate how standardised ML-DSA and hybrid signing transfer to a production HSM environment, using target-hardware measurements and integration constraints.

## Our inference
- This suggests a useful division of work: Nikolić could lead reproducible benchmarking and leakage screening, while Kriptolog could lead firmware integration and client-facing migration decisions. This joint-project proposal is conditional because her current availability is unknown, despite openness to this format.
- Finalist-era Dilithium measurements do not validate standardised ML-DSA on KL-HSM. Likewise, hybrid key exchange does not establish hybrid-signature compatibility. Actual-target signing and downstream verification tests are therefore likely to be more informative than extrapolating published performance.
- A narrowly scoped firmware experiment could reveal whether existing hardware supports migration or whether architectural changes are needed. Early interface and certification-impact review would likely reduce the risk of treating a functional prototype as production-ready.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose an eight-week first experiment on an agreed representative KL-HSM, using non-production keys and its production toolchain. Kriptolog would supply hardware, builds, its prototype and representative signing workloads; Nikolić would lead porting and measurement. Compare current RSA/ECDSA, standardised ML-DSA, and agreed hybrid signing with downstream verification. Use standard test vectors and separate tuning/evaluation workload samples, checking their independence. Measure correctness, peak RAM, flash, signing latency, throughput, signature size and timing leakage. Negotiate acceptance limits before testing: all correctness vectors pass, zero memory-budget overruns, performance within agreed workload-specific limits, and a screening target of |t| below 4.5 under an agreed protocol. Go/no-go should use the best configuration tested, not require hybrid to outperform alternatives; require successful hybrid interoperability, feasible hardware access, and a resourced twelve-month delivery plan. Final project acceptance would require a versioned roadmap with measured trade-offs and at least one working integrated proof of concept within twelve months.
- If the feasibility gate passes, propose months three through twelve for key-storage and signature-flow integration, hybrid packaging, verifier acceptance and downgrade-prevention tests. Map architecture changes to PKCS#11 and assess implications for the current Common Criteria evaluation configuration. Draft the versioned roadmap with Kriptolog; distinguish experimental support, production-readiness work and certification milestones.
- Propose regression tests across compiler settings and targeted side-channel and fault-injection evaluation before any production recommendation. Document residual risks and security/performance costs of mitigations. Use actual KL-HSM hardware first; consider an SLE97 route only if justified and hardware access is agreed. Neither initial leakage screening nor the proof of concept would constitute certification.
