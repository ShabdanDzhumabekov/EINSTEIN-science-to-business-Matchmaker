# Joint project: reliable cross-script contract search in KlozuraPro

## Verified facts
- The company estimates that script mismatch affected roughly one in five searches in a January 2024 server-log sample. This is a historical estimate, not a current evaluation baseline. [F1]
- The company wants robust mixed-script document search and identifies ambiguous Latin-to-Cyrillic mappings dependent on dialect conventions. [F2]
- The company uses PostgreSQL full-text search and wants no noticeable search-response slowdown. [F3]
- Task-specific company capacity is one backend engineer for up to six weeks; broader collaborative capacity is roughly one external project alongside product development. [F4]
- Dragić is open to joint projects with Serbian-document companies. His notes report two active projects and capacity for one smaller industrial contract or consultancy; he is not interested in general software development without research. [F5]
- Dragić prefers a clearly defined, sufficiently large Serbian-language corpus. The company describes its internal information-security policy as ISO 27001-aligned and self-assessed. [F6]
- Dragić’s work evaluates phoneme-based canonicalization on annotated court-decision retrieval pairs. His latency study reports a transformer normalizer adding 47 ms at the 99th percentile: an accuracy–latency tradeoff relevant to implementation risk. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could identify where normalization helps contract retrieval without unacceptable relevance or latency costs, and reveal whether implementation fits the available engineering window.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and approved access, Dragić could test how normalization placement transfers to PostgreSQL contract search, using adjudicated examples of mixed-script and ambiguous retrieval.

## Our inference
- These methods suggest a focused joint project comparing normalization placement rather than building a replacement search engine. Court-decision results do not establish performance on contracts or PostgreSQL; relevance and production-like latency need direct testing.
- Canonicalization could conflate distinct terms, particularly ambiguous sequences and names. Exception-focused judgments would likely distinguish genuine retrieval gains from misleading extra hits.
- The proposal is conditional: Dragić is open to this format, but his start date and weekly availability remain unconfirmed and his capacity is limited. Corpus access and annotation support also need agreement.

## Proposed next steps
- Run a controlled PostgreSQL experiment on approved contract–query samples, including opposite-script, mixed-script, ambiguous-sequence and same-script cases. Separate tuning and evaluation samples and check independence. Proposed matrix: indexing normalization off/query normalization off; on/off; off/on; on/on, holding ranking and morphology fixed. Have legal reviewers label intended documents and result relevance, including randomly sampled queries with and without hits; logs alone are not ground truth. Measure verified script-mismatch zero-hit rate, known-document retrieval, precision@10, p95/p99 latency under representative load, and index size. Proposed go/no-go targets for the best configuration, whichever wins: halve verified cross-script zero-hit failures, no precision@10 regression, and at most 5% p95/p99 slowdown, including mixed-script and ambiguous strata. Negotiate these targets as the meaning of robust and unnoticeable before testing. Require working PostgreSQL integration within the engineer’s six-week limit and approved access; a null single-factor result narrows, rather than ends, investigation.
- Subject to agreement, the company engineer would prepare the PostgreSQL baseline, controlled configurations and load replay; company legal reviewers would adjudicate relevance. Dragić would propose canonicalization rules, exception handling and evaluation design. Establish an acceptable corpus size and confidential access arrangements before extracting data; do not assume additional ML-engineering support.
- Propose week one for access, sampling and target agreement; weeks two through four for implementation and tuning; week five for held-out relevance and load tests; and week six for a decision and handover. Record migration effort, index growth, exception maintenance and unresolved failures, keeping neural fallback outside the initial scope unless feasible.
