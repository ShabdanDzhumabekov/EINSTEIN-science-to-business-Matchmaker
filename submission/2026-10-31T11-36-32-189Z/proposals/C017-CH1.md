# Targeted sampling of small salinity pockets on Tisa floodplain fields

## Verified facts
- TloCare reports that its four-hectare sampling grid misses salinity pockets of 0.3–0.8 hectares. Blanket densification increases fieldwork, laboratory costs and delivery time; the company wants to predict variable zones before dispatching its sampling team. [F1]
- TloCare reports GPS coordinates and laboratory results for about 18,000 points over ten years. Its listed routine outputs cover pH, phosphorus, potassium, humus and liming needs, rather than explicitly establishing an archive of salinity reference measurements. [F2]
- TloCare reports poor early-spring NDVI guidance because salinity was not clearly visible. It describes field sensing as a planned introduction within two years, not as established company equipment. [F3]
- TloCare has two programmers and one GIS specialist who can devote up to one-third of their working time to new functions. Its existing environment includes PostGIS and a Django portal backend. [F4]
- Kosanović is open to joint company projects and prefers genuine field collection. His EM38 and pXRF equipment is available to external partners subject to scheduling and consumables cost recovery. [F5]
- Kosanović’s published work applied regression-kriging to a commercial sunflower farm using EM38 conductivity and pXRF sodium covariates. This supports testing sensor-assisted zoning, rather than assuming that EM38 alone reproduces the published approach. [F6]
- His sample-reduction study reports unstable nugget estimates below 30 samples, compromising kriging weights. His flooded-field study reports that transition zones require repeat measurements. Both findings identify risks to reducing sampling and relying on a single survey. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The pilot could establish whether targeted sensing finds the pockets its grid misses without increasing routine service cost or delivery time, and identify when repeat surveys undermine that case.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement, the project could provide floodplain field measurements for testing small-pocket detection, sensor transfer and calibration-sample tradeoffs under a commercial sampling constraint.

## Our inference
- This suggests a plausible joint project, conditional on confirming Kosanović’s project availability, which remains unknown. Local calibration is essential: results elsewhere in Vojvodina do not establish detection of TloCare’s small floodplain pockets or explain sunflower losses on these fields.
- An initial conductivity survey could guide later targeted sampling, but likely cannot meet a literal requirement for recommendations before any field visit. Historical records should not be treated as current salinity ground truth without checking analytes, depths, dates and methods.
- A lightweight GIS workflow likely fits better than a new software platform. Savings remain uncertain because sensor access, calibration, repeat surveys and integration may offset fewer laboratory analyses.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a pilot on previously investigated problem fields. TloCare would supply field access, records and workflow costs; Kosanović would design sensing, calibration and validation. Compare the current four-hectare grid, EM38-assisted regression-kriging and EM38-plus-pXRF regression-kriging. Separate tuning and spatially held-out evaluation samples, checking independence. Obtain laboratory references across suspected pockets and randomly selected predicted-positive and predicted-negative areas; count uncertain cases separately. Measure confirmed-pocket recall at 0.3–0.8 hectares, false-positive area, salinity error, uncertainty, total operational cost per hectare and turnaround, separating one-off validation expenditure. Negotiable go/no-go targets for the best configuration, whichever it is, are at least 90% confirmed-pocket recall, no higher operational cost and no slower delivery than baseline, with targeted—not blanket—routine densification. Proceed only if recommendations meet the agreed pre-dispatch interpretation, repeat-survey costs are included, and implementation fits stated staff capacity. Otherwise revise or stop deployment.
- Propose an eight-week pilot after access and scheduling are agreed: audit records and protocols first, then field collection, modelling and held-out evaluation. Agree salinity definitions and reference-map resolution before collection. Check moisture and flooding conditions, repeating transition-zone measurements if feasible; any claim of seasonal reuse would remain conditional on further testing.
- After evaluation, test reduced calibration designs without reusing held-out references for tuning. Propose delivery of sampling coordinates, salinity zones and uncertainty layers into PostGIS, with a minimal portal view. Estimate integration effort, equipment charges and maintenance before committing to deployment.

## Open questions
- Can Kosanović confirm availability for the joint project’s fieldwork and analysis, including instrument scheduling and charges?
- Can TloCare authorise pilot-field access, independent reference sampling and an audit of archived analytes, depths, dates, methods and data-sharing permissions?
- What salinity definition, missed-pocket tolerance, false-positive tolerance, cost ceiling and delivery deadline should replace or confirm the proposed decision targets?
- Does pre-dispatch prediction permit an initial sensor campaign reused later, or must recommendations rely entirely on existing data?
