# Explainable, Site-Specific Anomaly Alerts for ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec reports no traffic labelled malicious and acknowledges that historical compromises could have gone unnoticed. Its archives therefore have no confirmed clean status. [F1]
- ProtoSense inspects Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP. ArmaturaSec reports that its previous public-dataset-trained classifier generated enough false positives for clients to switch alerts off. [F2]
- ArmaturaSec reports having on-device inference hardware and using custom Yocto Embedded Linux. Its hardware plan describes a dedicated security processor intended to avoid affecting packet-forwarding latency; that plan does not establish present availability. [F3]
- ArmaturaSec offers one to two part-time engineers and archive, bench and firmware access under NDA. Kiss's group describes university testbed access under a data-sharing agreement. [F4]
- Kiss's Modbus research constructed per-register statistical baselines on substation-testbed traffic. Her participation material states openness to joint projects with ICS monitoring companies. [F5]
- Kiss's incremental modelling study detected a planned process reconfiguration as a persistent anomaly episode and tested an operator-acknowledged reset mechanism. [F6]
- Her Isolation Forest study reports a calibration risk: an order-of-magnitude contamination mismatch reduced average precision from 0.91 to 0.63. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could show whether site-specific alerts are tolerable, understandable and deployable on actual ProtoSense hardware, while identifying whether BACnet needs further development.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permissions, Kiss could test how calibration and guarded adaptation transfer across protocols using operational archives and controlled bench scenarios.

## Our inference
- This joint-project proposal is conditional because Kiss's availability and team allocation remain unknown; openness to this format does not establish a commitment.
- Field-level baselines suggest a plausible route to plain-language explanations. However, the supplied research does not establish BACnet effectiveness or ProtoSense hardware fit; both require direct testing rather than extrapolation from Modbus or other ARM devices.
- The reported contamination sensitivity suggests that unlabelled calibration could produce misleading thresholds. Seasonal variation and adaptation could also hide persistent anomalies; bench success would not establish real-attack effectiveness or malicious intent.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose an isolated first experiment using representative site archives, separate chronological training/tuning/evaluation periods, and authorised injected attacks plus legitimate changes for each protocol. Check temporal and device leakage before evaluation; unavailable representative BACnet evaluation data blocks a decision. Compare fixed-threshold rules with site-specific statistical baselines, calibrated thresholds and operator-gated adaptation; train and calibrate without attack labels. Select the best configuration on tuning data, then evaluate once on held-out data separately for Modbus and BACnet. Proposed go/no-go targets, subject to negotiation: the upper 95% confidence bound on false-alert episodes is at most one per site-day; at least 80% of injected episodes are detected within 60 seconds; each participating plant manager correctly interprets at least four of five explanation tasks per protocol. Also require measured CPU, memory and storage within agreed appliance limits and no measurable forwarding-latency increase at agreed resolution under peak replay load. Insufficient exposure, unresolved permissions or failed protocol-specific checks prevent a pass, whichever configuration wins.
- Propose counting alerts for the same site, device and protocol separated by at most 60 seconds as one episode; review every episode, conservatively counting unresolved ones as false. Exposure is complete monitored site-hours divided by 24. Predefine confidence estimation and exposure adequacy before testing. Randomly expert-label 10% of all evaluation windows, each with equal inclusion probability regardless of alerts, to audit contamination—not estimate episode counts. Measure detection only on injected or confirmed cases.
- Propose six weeks after approvals: two for permissions, hardware profiling and sampling; two for implementation and tuning; two for locked evaluation and review. Kiss could lead features, calibration and evaluation; ArmaturaSec's part-time engineers could lead replay, Yocto integration and profiling. Keep updates operator-approved and test persistent anomalies before permitting adaptation. Scope to available capacity, with NDA and university data-sharing terms settled first.
