# Reducing Serbian Support-Search Misses Without Full Re-indexing

## Verified facts
- LinguaBot reports that about 18% of 12,000 real support queries from one bank client returned no useful result; more than half of those misses were attributed to morphology or script confusion. Users also mix scripts within words. [F1]
- The company currently uses Elasticsearch with a Croatian-oriented stemmer. It reports that manually maintaining synonym lists does not scale with quarterly terminology changes and identifies vocabulary updating as an indexing concern. [F2]
- LinguaBot reports around 85,000 labelled query-response pairs across all clients and two in-house linguists able to annotate or validate solutions. [F3]
- LinguaBot describes approximately EUR 8,000 for tooling or data work. Its stated success definition concerns both retrieval misses and ongoing maintenance. [F4]
- Dragić’s research investigates interactions between Serbian stemming or lemmatisation and script normalization. His canonicalization work maps tokens to phoneme sequences for retrieval. [F5]
- Dragić lists openness to relevant company joint projects. His notes describe two active projects and capacity for one smaller industrial contract or consulting engagement; they also distinguish research collaboration from general software development. [F6]
- His neural transliteration study reports a tradeoff: character error on regular tokens was 0.4% for the transformer versus 0.1% for deterministic rules, despite better exception handling. His phoneme-based retrieval evaluation used legal documents, not customer-support queries. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could identify which normalization changes reduce real support-search misses and whether vocabulary updates remain practical without rebuilding the full index.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permissions, the project could provide support-query material for studying morphology–script interactions and transfer beyond legal and news retrieval.

## Our inference
- This suggests a focused joint research project could distinguish morphology failures from script failures and test their interaction. Script normalization alone is unlikely to cover the entire problem, while stronger normalization could wrongly merge distinct product names.
- Transfer to short banking and telecom queries, new product names and within-word typos remains unvalidated. Incremental document ingestion would not itself demonstrate compatibility after normalization-rule or vocabulary changes; maintenance effort may persist.
- This proposal is conditional: joint-project willingness is open, but Dragić’s start date and time allocation are unconfirmed and his stated capacity is limited.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a six-month experiment using permission-cleared query-response data, knowledge-base snapshots and held-out bank queries. LinguaBot would provide integration and linguist review; Dragić would design normalization and analysis. Verify tuning/evaluation independence against duplicate-query, session and temporal leakage. Hold corpus and ranking fixed; compare baseline, Serbian morphology alone, phoneme canonicalization alone, and both, defining each factor independently. Test preprocessing order on tuning data; freeze the best configuration, whichever it is. Go/no-go would require a below-5% miss rate, with upper 95% confidence bound below 5%, on the original bank population separately before and after vocabulary updates. Also require Elasticsearch integration, zero full-index rebuilds during updates, agreed precision/latency/maintenance limits, tooling/data costs within approximately EUR 8,000, and a testable prototype within six months. Missing evidence means no pass. Separately test telecom transfer, inflections, product names and within-word mixing; a null single-factor result would not end interaction testing.
- Propose linguist review of every evaluation query at an agreed retrieval depth. Count one miss per submitted query instance when no returned answer is useful; use all evaluated query instances as denominator, retain repeated submissions and count unresolved judgments as misses. Report bank and telecom results separately. Validate existing query-response labels for retrieval relevance rather than assuming suitability; use controlled variants only as diagnostic tests.
- Propose month one for permissions, baseline replication and split auditing; months two–three for tuning; months four–five for untouched evaluation and vocabulary-update replay; month six for review. Log update time, linguist hours, precision and latency under agreed load. Test query-time expansion or compatible incremental updates. Agree numeric maintenance, precision and latency limits before testing; clarify research fees and infrastructure funding separately.
