# Cold-store weld cracking in conveyor frames

## Verified facts
- AutomVoj reports conveyor frame weld-joint cracking after roughly 18 months in chilled environments, with cold stores typically operating between 2 °C and 6 °C. [F1]
- The company has replaced 14 assemblies under warranty this year and cannot reproduce the failure because it tests at room temperature; it states it lacks equipment or know-how for low-temperature cyclic-load testing. [F2]
- AutomVoj has two failed sections in storage plus site photos and measurements, and the frames are welded from mild steel hollow section and painted; its fabrication technology includes mild steel MIG/MAG welded frame fabrication. [F3]
- Erić's research group focuses on fatigue and fracture mechanics of welded structural steel joints under combined cyclic mechanical loading and low-temperature thermal excursions, and routinely uses servo-hydraulic fatigue machines, a climate chamber reaching −60 °C, and Abaqus FE models. [F4]
- Erić's publication reports that superimposed thermal cycling between −30 °C and +20 °C at 0.5 cycles per hour during mechanical fatigue loading alters crack initiation and growth behaviour in butt-welded S275JR plates. [F5]
- In S355 cruciform fillet-welded joints, initial peak longitudinal residual stress averaged +287 MPa in the as-welded state, and after 1000 cycles at 180 MPa surface residual stresses had relaxed to +61 MPa. [F6]
- Erić's climate chamber and servo-hydraulic machine are shared with two other research groups, and scheduling access for extended test campaigns requires advance coordination; AutomVoj has no dedicated R&D department, and technical development happens informally through project work. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The first experiment could tell AutomVoj which of cold temperature, condensation or vibration is associated with weld-toe cracking and whether its room-temperature pass is insufficient, giving a basis to decide which welding or design change to trial next; it would not by itself stop warranty replacements.
- For the researcher: For Erić, the experiment could test whether thermo-mechanical fatigue and residual-stress methods transfer to painted mild-steel hollow-section conveyor T-joints in a wet 2–6 °C regime, using company failed sections and production specimens conditional on agreement. This proposal is conditional because Erić's availability for an extended low-temperature campaign is unknown and must be confirmed; his joint-project willingness is recorded as open.

## Our inference
- Erić's thermal-cycling and residual-stress work suggests the group's methods are relevant, but the evidence is for S275JR butt welds and S355 cruciform joints, not AutomVoj's painted mild-steel hollow-section T-joints in a wet 2–6 °C cold store; the first experiment must test that transfer gap rather than assume it.
- Because AutomVoj suspects thermal cycling, condensation and vibration act together, a factorial design that changes one factor at a time plus a combined condition is likely to show which factor matters; a factor with no effect alone would narrow, not close, the mechanism search.
- Shared chamber and machine access plus the absence of a dedicated R&D department suggest scheduling and specimen preparation are likely critical-path items, so a named company technical lead and a staged campaign are prudent.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment: build a factorial coupon campaign on crossmember-to-stringer T-joints made by AutomVoj from its painted mild-steel hollow-section MIG/MAG construction, plus metallographic and fractographic examination of the two failed sections. Baseline is dry cyclic mechanical loading at room temperature; single-factor conditions add cold 2–6 °C, condensation/wetness, or vibration separately, and one condition combines cold, wetness and vibration. Measure crack initiation (e.g. 5% stiffness drop) and growth at the weld toe, with residual-stress and FE support. The Erić group would provide the shared climate chamber, servo-hydraulic testing and analysis; AutomVoj would provide specimens, failed sections, site records and a named technical lead. Go/no-go: reproduce weld-toe cracking or a statistically resolvable initiation-life reduction in the company's 2–6 °C wet condition and identify at least one factor; if not, revise load and environment histories before any welding-procedure change. Proposed 12-month timeline: specimen preparation and characterisation months 1–2, testing months 3–8, analysis and recommendation months 9–12.
- Before or alongside testing, section and examine the two failed sections: fractography, metallography, hardness, weld-toe geometry and residual-stress measurement, compared with lab-made coupons and the site photos and measurements. AutomVoj would supply weld procedure, filler metal, joint geometry, paint records and service histories if available. A pre-agreed match/no-match checklist against service crack location and appearance should decide whether lab conditions are representative; uncertain evidence is recorded as unresolved rather than counted as confirmation.
- If Phase 1 isolates a factor, run a second small matrix comparing current construction with candidate welding-procedure, filler-material and joint-design variants under the worst representative condition. Proposed success criterion for going to a company pilot: a pre-agreed, statistically resolvable reduction in crack initiation or growth versus current construction, measured at 2–6 °C with condensation present; proposed timeline 6 months after Phase 1. These are negotiation targets, not guaranteed outcomes.
