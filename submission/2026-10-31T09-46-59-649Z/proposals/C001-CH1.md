# Unsupervised SPC drift detection for Modbus/BACnet traffic

## Verified facts
- The company says it has zero examples of malicious traffic and no client packet capture containing a confirmed attack, so supervised classifiers are not usable for its need. [F1]
- The collaboration would rely on access to the company's traffic archives, hardware bench and firmware under NDA; the researcher is interested in labelled or time-stamped operational data from real production systems. [F2]
- The target is Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP traffic, as handled by ProtoSense, rather than generic sensor streams. [F3]
- The company requires a method that works with no attack examples and can be explained to a plant manager in plain language; past false positives led clients to switch alerts off. [F4]
- Deployment must support on-device anomaly detection without affecting packet forwarding latency, while the researcher can only support lightweight deployment of pre-trained models, not extensive hardware or firmware work. [F5]
- ArmaturaSec can dedicate one to two engineers part-time to a collaborative project. [F6]
- The researcher's open_to entry includes joint research projects with companies operating sensor-instrumented production equipment. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The first experiment could show whether a site-specific statistical baseline can flag Modbus/BACnet drift without labelled attacks and whether alerts can be expressed in plant-manager language; it may also indicate whether the false-alarm problem that led clients to switch alerts off is reduced.
- For the researcher: For Ostojić, the experiment would test whether SPC/change-point methods validated on sensor streams transfer to protocol-level traffic and, conditional on NDA and availability, give her time-stamped multi-site captures to study.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment: on a held-out sample of presumed-normal Modbus/BACnet captures from at least two consenting sites, build protocol-level features (function-code mix, request/response timing, register and object access patterns) and fit an unsupervised SPC/change-point baseline; compare with a simple fixed-threshold baseline. Use separate tuning and evaluation windows and have the company check their independence. Inject confirmed synthetic anomalies via the PLC simulators (e.g., unauthorized write, polling-rate change) and count detection only on these injected cases; treat unlabelled archives as presumed normal. Measure false alarms per site-day on normal windows, detection delay on injections, and whether each alert can be rendered as a plain-language statement of changed behaviour. Go/no-go: false alarms within a company-agreed limit on normal evaluation windows, detection on injected cases, and a plant engineer judging sample alerts explainable; otherwise stop or redesign. Researcher proposes feature/method design and analysis; company proposes archive access, injection setup and engineering review.
- Proposed timeline and success criteria for negotiation: weeks 1-2 data/NDA and feature specification; weeks 3-6 first experiment; weeks 7-10 shadow-mode evaluation if go. Targets to test: false alarms below an agreed per-site-day limit, detection of all injected confirmed cases with acceptable delay, and plant-manager explanation accepted on a sample.
- Define an evaluation protocol that separates tuning and evaluation samples, labels evaluation windows by expert review of a sample plus injected confirmed cases, counts uncertain cases explicitly, and checks independence; company and researcher sign off before modelling.
