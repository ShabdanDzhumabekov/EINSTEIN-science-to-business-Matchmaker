# Explainable, Site-Specific Anomaly Detection for ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec reports several terabytes of client captures collected over three years, but no confirmed attack captures. Some clients lacked monitoring, so the company acknowledges that compromise could have gone unnoticed. [F1]
- ProtoSense covers Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP. ArmaturaSec reports that its previous classifier's false positives caused clients to disable alerts. Its account discusses site-specific baselines and plain-language communication with plant managers. [F2]
- The company reports hardware for on-device inference and an Embedded Linux, C and Rust environment. Its hardware plan discusses anomaly detection and packet-forwarding latency. [F3]
- ArmaturaSec states a collaboration capacity of one to two part-time engineers and identifies traffic archives, a hardware bench and its firmware codebase within an NDA framework. [F4]
- Kiss’s Modbus research models per-register timing, values and command sequences. Her incremental modelling work also tested operator-acknowledged resets after planned process reconfiguration. [F5]
- Kiss’s group operates an ICS testbed with Modbus/TCP and DNP3 traffic generators. Her notes identify a data-sharing agreement as the framework for industrial testbed access. [F6]
- Kiss lists joint projects with ICS monitoring companies among her collaboration interests. Her review discusses a false-alarm versus response-latency tradeoff. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The pilot could establish whether site-specific detection produces manageable, understandable alerts on both protocols without compromising appliance operation, and identify unresolved deployment barriers.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement, Kiss could investigate transfer from established Modbus methods to BACnet and operational hardware, using authorised longitudinal captures and controlled bench scenarios to study calibration, drift and explanation quality.

## Our inference
- This suggests a practical joint project combining interpretable statistical baselines with one-class modelling. Participation remains conditional because Kiss’s availability is unknown, despite her stated openness to joint projects.
- The supplied work does not establish BACnet validity or ProtoSense hardware compatibility. Transfer testing is therefore central; injected scenarios cannot establish real-world attack recall.
- Unnoticed compromise could contaminate training, while maintenance could trigger nuisance alerts. Automatic adaptation might absorb persistent attacks; restrictive updates could increase alert burden. The reported false-alarm versus response-latency tradeoff is a deployment risk.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a six-week pilot: ArmaturaSec supplies authorised captures, bench replay and firmware integration; Kiss designs features and evaluation. Separate presumed-normal, unlabelled training, tuning and later held-out periods; verify independence and operating-mode coverage. Compare fixed-threshold rules, statistical baselines and one-class models, training and calibrating without attack labels. Select the best configuration using tuning data, whichever method wins. Evaluate separately for Modbus and BACnet on held-out client replay and controlled bench anomalies, including BACnet commands and legitimate changes. Proposed go/no-go requires the statistical prerequisites below: at least 80% of fully annotated injected episodes detected within 60 seconds; the burden limits below; at least 80% correct plant-manager identification of changed behaviour and appropriate next action; agreed CPU, RAM, storage and throughput limits; and no measurable forwarding-latency increase under representative peak load at agreed resolution. Benchmark the actual Yocto/C/Rust appliance, not presumed hardware fit. Missing protocol data, permissions or sufficient evidence leaves acceptance unresolved.
- Propose counting all alert episodes, merging flagged non-overlapping ten-second windows within site/protocol across gaps at most 60 seconds. Require upper 95% day-block-bootstrap bounds of one episode per observed site/protocol-day and, additionally, 1% flagged windows per observed windows, separately per protocol. The latter prevents persistent alerting disappearing through merging. Retain unresolved alerts in both numerators. Predefine sufficient exposure and independent day blocks; insufficient evidence cannot pass. These burden measures use all alerts, not sampled labels.
- Randomly sample all evaluation windows uniformly, including non-alert windows, with inclusion probability n/N, for expert review; unresolved labels remain potentially anomalous, not clean. Sampling does not estimate the all-alert burden numerator. Restrict detection claims to fully annotated injected or confirmed cases. Require operator approval for baseline resets and test whether persistent injected anomalies are silently learned away. Negotiate NDA, client permissions and testbed data-sharing terms within the stated part-time capacity before execution.

## Open questions
- Can both parties preregister detection scoring separately per protocol: one-to-one matching of each fully annotated injected episode to a new alert beginning within 60 seconds of onset, unmatched episodes counted as misses, and a one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of at least 80%, using independent-run cluster resampling, with minimum runs and episodes fixed beforehand and insufficient evidence unable to pass?
- Can representative plant managers undertake explanation testing separately per protocol, scoring each distinct user-case first response as correct only when both changed behaviour and appropriate next action match a preregistered rubric, incomplete answers as failures, repeats excluded, and requiring a one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of at least 80% using user-and-case clustered resampling, with minimum independent users and cases agreed beforehand and insufficient evidence unable to pass?
- Can Kiss confirm availability, implementation responsibilities, and a feasible budget and six-week schedule, and can ArmaturaSec nominate expert reviewers and plant managers and confirm or revise the proposed burden, detection-delay and explanation criteria before preregistration?
- Which appliance, resource limits, peak traffic conditions and latency measurement resolution will define acceptance, and can authorised captures for both protocols, documented operating changes and BACnet-capable bench equipment be made available under agreed confidentiality and data-sharing terms?
