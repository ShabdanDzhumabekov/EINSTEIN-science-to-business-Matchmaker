# Explaining chilled-store conveyor weld cracking

## Verified facts
- AutomVoj reports cracking after about 18 months at main crossmember-to-stringer connections in painted mild-steel hollow-section frames. Its room-temperature loaded workshop tests have not reproduced the failure. [F1]
- The company describes cold-store operation at 2–6 °C and suspects interacting thermal cycling, condensation and loaded-belt vibration; it does not report established causal roles. [F2]
- AutomVoj has site photographs and crack measurements, but only two retained failed sections. [F3]
- AutomVoj lacks low-temperature cyclic-load testing equipment and know-how. Erić’s climate chamber and servo-hydraulic machine are shared with two other research groups. [F4]
- AutomVoj has no dedicated R&D department; its chief engineer and two senior automation engineers divide their time between live projects and improvement work. [F5]
- Erić’s published work examined mechanical fatigue combined with thermal cycling in butt-welded S275JR plates between −30 °C and +20 °C. It reported crack growth exceeding simple mechanical-plus-thermal predictions. [F6]
- Erić lists openness to joint projects involving low-temperature or combined-loading fatigue testing. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The first comparison could distinguish individual and interacting service factors and provide a defensible basis for changing the joint rather than relying on room-temperature tests alone.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and access, Erić could test transfer of thermo-mechanical fatigue methods to coated hollow-section conveyor joints using service measurements, failed-section evidence and controlled comparisons.

## Our inference
- This suggests a relevant joint project, conditional on Erić’s unknown availability and AutomVoj’s agreement. Openness does not establish a commitment or equipment booking.
- Transfer remains unvalidated: colder plate tests do not establish the mechanism in painted hollow-section joints at 2–6 °C. Condensation control and corrosion expertise may require additional support; accelerated loading could produce irrelevant fractures and cannot alone establish an 18-month service life.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose production-representative painted crossmember-to-stringer specimens: AutomVoj would contribute fabrication records, specimens and service measurements; Erić would lead examination and testing, subject to agreement. Independently define thermal cycling (steady 2–6 °C chilled condition versus measured excursions), condensation (dry versus controlled wetting) and vibration (absent versus measured spectrum). Hold static belt load, weld condition and coating comparable. Test baseline, each factor alone, all pairs and the full combination, plus the room-temperature workshop reference. Separate tuning from held-out evaluation specimens and verify independence. Measure cycles to a predefined detectable crack, growth, origin and morphology. Select the best-supported condition on tuning data. Proposed reproduction gate: at least two of three held-out replicates must match the field failure under the frozen rubric and exposure window, with measured exposures reproduced and uncertainty reported. Ambiguous matches and specimens without matching cracks by the window’s end do not count. Mechanism support additionally requires the held-out contrasts below; otherwise reproduction is inconclusive. An inactive single factor never excludes interactions.
- Propose two weeks for evidence review and instrumentation planning, then six weeks of testing after advance equipment booking; renegotiate against measured service cycles. Before held-out testing, freeze a field-informed rubric specifying connection location, origin, morphology and tolerances, ambiguous-match handling, and exposure/censoring window. Preserve failed material through non-destructive documentation and an agreed sampling map. Confirm representative-specimen fit, independent moisture control, safe operation and engineering allocation before manufacture; insufficient evidence to freeze these prerequisites blocks evaluation.
- Prespecify held-out main-effect and interaction contrasts, meaningful-effect bounds and simultaneous 95% confidence intervals. Support a factor explanation only when a contrast interval lies wholly beyond its negligible-effect band; indistinguishable conditions or insufficient precision remain inconclusive. After both gates pass, use calibrated modelling to tune welding, filler or geometry changes. Evaluate the best configuration independently against unchanged production joints under reproduced exposures, using pre-agreed adoption criteria and welding requirements. Warranty reduction remains a longer-term field-validation question.
