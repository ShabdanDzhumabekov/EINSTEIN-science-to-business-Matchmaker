# Testing PassengerView ETA Accuracy for Romanian Bus Networks

## Verified facts
- The company reports a contractual benchmark of displayed ETAs within 90 seconds of actual arrival at least 85% of the time. Its current PassengerView uses the published timetable plus a last-position offset. [F1]
- The company collects ten-second GPS pings from 340 buses. Its PostgreSQL archive contains roughly 2.8 billion rows, and weather data is not integrated. [F2]
- The company's environment includes Java 17/Spring Boot and on-premise Docker/Kubernetes. It offers a senior backend engineer and domain specialist part-time; its Python-capable data engineer lacks time-series modelling experience. [F3]
- The company's stated implementation window is twelve months from approval, originally expected in Q3 2024. Novaković's notes describe moderate workload and capacity for one new collaboration in 2026. [F4]
- The company describes historical AVL and timetable access through a signed data-sharing agreement. Novaković expresses a preference to publish anonymised results. [F5]
- Novaković's published work develops Kalman-filter travel-time estimation from AVL. Her cross-network study reports topology-dependent transfer limits; her recurrent-model study reports weaker evening-peak performance on shared congested corridors. [F6]
- Novaković lists joint projects with urban-mobility software companies among her collaboration interests. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could establish whether its existing data supports the contractual ETA benchmark for each Romanian customer and identify whether data quality, modelling or deployment is the main obstacle.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement, the project could provide multi-network AVL material to test transfer of state-space and recurrent models under congestion and operational serving constraints.

## Our inference
- Her AVL modelling experience suggests a strong methodological fit, but results from other networks and error metrics do not establish Romanian contractual performance. Congestion remains a specific transfer risk.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional: current availability remains unconfirmed, and the stated 2026 capacity may conflict with the company's original implementation schedule.
- A bounded extraction and replay study likely offers a more practical starting point than processing the entire archive or assuming that a research prototype is production-ready.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose an eight-week replay experiment after prerequisites are resolved. Company staff would extract Romanian AVL/timetable samples and validate arrivals; Novaković would develop locally calibrated state-space and GRU candidates. Compare against current PassengerView using prediction-time information only. Separate tuning and later held-out periods by municipality, checking independence and leakage; select the configuration on tuning data. Count each agreed eligible display update once, without merging; denominator is all eligible updates during observed service periods. Score absolute arrival error ≤90 seconds using validated arrivals; conservatively count unresolved outcomes and missing predictions as failures. Report census counts, service-day uncertainty, and separate peak/disruption results. Proposed go/no-go: the best tuning-selected configuration, including baseline, reaches ≥85% separately for every customer's holdout, with a service-day confidence lower bound ≥85%, plus feasible integration within the remaining twelve-month window. Insufficient labels, customer coverage, or precision means no pass. Measure telemetry-to-display delay and serving cost against agreed deployment limits.
- Before extraction, sign an operator-authorised data-sharing agreement covering segregation, anonymisation, publication review and permitted outputs. Agree arrival definitions, prediction horizons and eligibility rules. Audit door events against independently checked arrivals and reconstruct historical timetables; do not treat unverified records as ground truth. Missing Romanian evaluation data remains an unresolved prerequisite.
- Propose weeks 1–2 for audit, weeks 3–5 for preprocessing and tuning, and weeks 6–8 for locked evaluation and integration profiling. Start without weather features; obtain confirmed disruption/weather annotations for stratified evaluation. Propose company-led Java/on-premise integration and researcher-led modelling handover, including fallback, retraining and monitoring procedures, rather than assuming existing production readiness.

## Open questions
- What are the actual approval date and remaining contractual window, and can Novaković confirm current availability, start date and effort?
- How do the contracts define actual arrival, prediction horizons, eligible display updates, cancellations, stale displays and municipality-level acceptance?
- Which Romanian customer histories, timetable versions and independently verifiable arrival/disruption records are available for separate tuning and held-out evaluation?
- What compute, latency, refresh-rate, budget and ownership limits apply, and can all parties authorise data access and acceptable anonymised publication terms?
