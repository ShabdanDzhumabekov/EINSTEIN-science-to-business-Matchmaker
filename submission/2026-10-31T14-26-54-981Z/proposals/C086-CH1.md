## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could distinguish a feasible firmware migration from changes requiring architectural redesign, giving clients a more defensible account of resource costs, compatibility and release dependencies.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and access, Nikolić could test how earlier signature implementation methods transfer to production HSM firmware, using target-hardware measurements to study resource, toolchain and security trade-offs.

## Our inference
- Her benchmarking experience suggests a strong methodological fit, but earlier Dilithium results cannot establish final ML-DSA correctness, resource fit or security on KL-HSM hardware. The published verification-memory versus signing-cost trade-off motivates separate signing-latency and memory measurements, not forecasts from published timings.
- Hybrid key-exchange experience likely helps architecture discussions, but signing policy, PKCS#11 integration and client verifier compatibility need separate validation. Compiler-dependent leakage is a material risk; resource optimisation could conflict with security hardening.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional because Nikolić's current availability is unknown. A narrowly bounded hardware pilot likely fits the company's collaborative capacity better than immediate product-wide migration.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose an eight-week experiment on one agreed KL-HSM variant with its production toolchain. Kriptolog would supply development hardware, builds, its prototype and non-production keys; Nikolić would lead porting and measurement. Compare current RSA/ECDSA with standards-conformant ML-DSA and a defined hybrid flow using known-answer vectors and representative signing workloads. Separate tuning and held-out evaluation workloads, verify independence, and select the best configuration on tuning data. Measure correctness, peak RAM, flash, p99 latency, throughput, PKCS#11/verifier interoperability and preliminary timing leakage. Proposed go/no-go requires all correctness/interoperability tests passing, resource use and performance within pre-agreed numerical limits under the frozen specification below, and no detected leakage in its adequately powered screen. Judge the best configuration against these criteria, not superiority over another method. Missing specifications or insufficient evidence mean unresolved, not pass. Include feasibility of the twelve-month delivery; functional success would not establish production security or certification.
- Before evaluation, freeze an acceptance specification defining workloads, operating conditions, repeats, numerical ceilings and floors, and confidence levels. Require the one-sided upper confidence bound for p99 latency below its ceiling and lower bound for throughput above its floor. Define the leakage test threshold, sample size, minimum detectable effect and positive-control sensitivity criterion. An insensitive screen cannot pass; unresolved specifications or inadequate precision block go/no-go. These procedures are proposals for agreement.
- Propose months three through twelve for integration and a versioned roadmap covering key storage, signature flows, hybrid policy, compatibility and measured trade-offs. Check delivery against the twelve-month outcome and firmware-version reporting needs. Bound effort, budget, access and confidentiality/licensing terms; prioritise KL-HSM hardware, scheduling justified SLE97 comparisons within two-kit availability or negotiating additional units. Include certification change-impact review and agreed side-channel/fault testing; unresolved requirements block production-readiness claims. Reconfirm scope and schedule after the pilot.
