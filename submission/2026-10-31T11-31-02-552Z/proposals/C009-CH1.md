# Serbian-aware retrieval with maintainable vocabulary updates

## Verified facts
- LinguaBot reports that, in a sample of 12,000 real support queries from one bank client, about 18% returned no useful result. [F1]
- The company's existing Elasticsearch setup uses a Croatian-oriented stemmer that misses Serbian forms. Its users mix Latin and Cyrillic, including within words; integration must therefore address both morphology and mixed-script inputs. [F2]
- LinguaBot says manual synonym-list maintenance does not scale with quarterly terminology additions. It requires vocabulary updates without a full re-index. [F3]
- LinguaBot has around 85,000 labelled query-response pairs across clients and two linguists who can annotate or validate solutions. Its stated target is a miss rate below 5%, sustained through manageable maintenance. [F4]
- The company's tooling/data-work budget is approximately EUR 8,000, and it wants something testable within six months. [F5]
- Nikolić's lemmatizer paper describes applying a unified internal representation before lemmatization and restoring the original script afterward. [F6]
- Her morphological-lexicon study reports 91.3% paradigm correctness for common vocabulary and 78.6% for borrowings. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could identify which changes reduce support-search misses and whether terminology updates remain practical without rebuilding the full index.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permitted access, the project could provide labelled support queries for testing how Serbian morphological methods transfer to commercial terminology and mixed-script inputs.

## Our inference
- This suggests a plausible joint project linking Serbian linguistic methods to retrieval engineering. The proposal is conditional: Nikolić's participation record indicates openness to joint projects, but her availability and committed effort remain unknown.
- Corpus results do not establish performance on banking or telecom support queries, product names or within-word script typos. Lower correctness on borrowings suggests a risk of incorrect expansions; normalization could also merge meaningful distinctions and reduce precision.

## Proposed next steps
- First experiment, proposed for months 1–3: reproduce the current Elasticsearch baseline using the 12,000-query sample where accessible. Create separate tuning and held-out evaluation samples from the labelled pairs, checking client, time and duplicate overlap before treating them as independent. Test an explicit matrix: current pipeline; added script normalization only; Serbian lemmatization only; both additions. Include banking, telecom, product-name and within-word-mixing strata. Proposed roles: LinguaBot supplies permitted data, integration and linguist review; Nikolić guides morphology and error analysis. Measure no-useful-result rate, retrieval precision, latency and linguist correction time. Replay new-vocabulary updates and measure re-index operations, effort and post-update miss rate. Go/no-go uses the best configuration tested, regardless of method: below 5% misses overall and after updates, acceptable stratum performance, agreed precision/latency and maintenance limits, no full re-index, and a testable prototype within six months and approximately EUR 8,000 tooling/data spend. These are negotiation targets; unresolved feasibility or unmet criteria trigger revision rather than deployment.
- During months 3–5, propose testing paradigm-based query expansion and an external vocabulary layer against the initial configurations. Validate borrowings and ambiguous forms with the linguists. A factor showing no isolated benefit would narrow, not close, the investigation; inspect remaining confirmed misses and test additional interactions.
- By month six, propose repeating the frozen evaluation after a terminology update and documenting update effort, regressions and costs. Before starting, agree data controls, commercial-use permissions, artifact access, staffing and responsibility for maintenance; tooling funds should not be assumed to cover research personnel.
