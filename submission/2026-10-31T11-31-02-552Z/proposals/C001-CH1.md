# Site-specific, explainable anomaly detection for ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec reports no malicious examples and acknowledges that historical compromises could have gone unnoticed. Its archives therefore cannot be assumed clean: training and operational calibration must work without attack-labelled examples, not depend on an attack-free guarantee. [F1]
- The company needs site-specific monitoring for both Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP. Its previous classifier generated false positives that led clients to disable alerts; explanations must be understandable to a plant manager. [F2]
- ArmaturaSec says it has on-device inference hardware and uses embedded Linux. Its hardware plan specifies anomaly detection without affecting packet-forwarding latency; actual pilot hardware budgets still need establishing. [F3]
- Company capacity is one to two part-time engineers, with archives, bench and firmware access under NDA. Use of Kiss's group testbed requires a data-sharing agreement. [F4]
- Kiss's Modbus research provides a concrete baseline method using register-level request timing, payload values and function-code sequences. This evidence concerns Modbus/TCP on a substation testbed, not validation on BACnet or ArmaturaSec appliances. [F5]
- Her incremental modelling work found that a planned process reconfiguration produced a persistent anomaly episode. This illustrates a maintenance-related nuisance-alert risk; the work proposed an operator-acknowledged reset. [F6]
- Kiss lists openness to joint projects with companies deploying ICS monitoring solutions. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The pilot could show whether useful, understandable alerts are achievable on its own devices without attack-labelled training, and identify whether BACnet support or nuisance alerts block deployment.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permissions, Kiss could test how site-specific baselines transfer to BACnet and contaminated operational archives, using permitted captures and controlled bench scenarios.

## Our inference
- A joint project appears well matched: protocol-specific statistical baselines could produce explanations linking an alert to changed timing, values or commands rather than an opaque attack classification.
- The proposal is conditional on confirming Kiss's availability, which is unknown. Openness to joint projects does not establish a commitment of effort.
- BACnet transfer, archive contamination and appliance performance remain experimental gaps. Bench detection could establish bounded scenario coverage, but would not establish recall against unknown operational attacks.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a six-week pilot using unlabelled client captures, chronological training/calibration/evaluation partitions and an unseen-site evaluation where feasible; check partition independence. Compare fixed-threshold rules with site-specific statistical or one-class models, training and calibrating without attack labels. Test Modbus and BACnet separately, including controlled bench anomalies, legitimate changes and sensitivity to possible baseline contamination. Proposed go/no-go targets for the best configuration, regardless of method: at most one false alert per site-day, at least 80% detection of reserved injected scenarios within 60 seconds, and plant managers correctly explaining at least 80% of sampled alerts. Require authorised access, Yocto on-device execution within agreed CPU/RAM limits, and no measurable forwarding-latency increase at agreed representative loads and measurement resolution. Freeze these negotiation targets before evaluation; report uncertainty and protocol-specific results. Proceed only if both protocols meet the agreed gates without requiring clean-archive guarantees or attack-labelled training/calibration; otherwise revise scope or stop.
- Propose that company engineers select permitted captures, implement feature extraction and profile the target appliance, while Kiss designs baselines and evaluation. Randomly sample across all evaluation windows, including windows with and without alerts, for expert labelling. Compute false-alert rates on reviewed normal windows, count uncertain cases separately, and measure detection only on confirmed or injected cases. Keep evaluation labels out of tuning.
- Subject to available effort, propose weeks one–two for permissions, hardware budgets, representative operating regimes and separate tuning/evaluation samples; weeks three–four for models and safe bench tests; weeks five–six for frozen evaluation and manager review. Test BACnet-specific features and scenarios explicitly, and compare legitimate-change handling with and without operator-authorised baseline updates rather than assuming Modbus results transfer.
