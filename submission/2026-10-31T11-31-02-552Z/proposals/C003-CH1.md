# Explaining cold-store conveyor weld cracking

## Verified facts
- AutomVoj reports cracking at main crossmember-to-stringer welds after about 18 months. Its frames use painted mild-steel hollow sections, operate in cold stores typically at 2–6 °C, and pass workshop room-temperature load tests. These conditions define the required transfer test. [F1]
- AutomVoj has site photographs, crack measurements and two failed sections in storage. Access and a sampling plan preserving material for complementary examinations are therefore prerequisites proposed for this investigation. [F2]
- AutomVoj lacks low-temperature cyclic-testing capability. Erić’s group uses fatigue machines and a climate chamber, but these are shared and extended campaigns require advance scheduling. Combined loading, representative fixtures and condensation control still need confirmation; equipment availability alone does not establish suitability. [F3]
- AutomVoj reports no dedicated R&D department; its chief engineer and two senior automation engineers divide time between live projects and improvement work. Named engineering support must therefore be negotiated rather than assumed. [F4]
- AutomVoj seeks a causal explanation that enables changes to welding procedure, filler material or joint design, rather than a fatigue-life calculation alone. [F5]
- Erić’s thermal-fatigue study compared isothermal and combined thermal–mechanical loading in butt-welded S275JR plates. It reported crack-growth behaviour exceeding simple-superposition predictions, supporting controlled comparisons but warning against additive damage assumptions. [F6]
- Erić lists joint projects involving low-temperature or combined-loading fatigue testing among his collaboration interests. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could distinguish service contributors missed by room-temperature tests and identify whether a specific manufacturing change merits validation, rather than selecting a remedy from an assumed mechanism.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and access, Erić could test whether thermal–mechanical fatigue methods transfer to painted conveyor connections, using recovered failures, service measurements and controlled comparative specimens.

## Our inference
- This suggests a relevant joint project, conditional on confirming Erić’s currently unknown personal availability and equipment slots; his stated openness is not a project commitment.
- Transfer remains unvalidated from colder plate-joint studies to painted hollow sections at 2–6 °C. Condensation capability may require external support, and accelerated tests could produce a different failure mechanism.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a first experiment using production-representative painted connections, with separate tuning and evaluation batches whose independence is checked. AutomVoj would supply specifications, specimens and site-monitoring support; Erić would lead failure examination, loading design and testing. Preserve portions of both recovered sections. Measure service strain, vibration, temperature and humidity before fixing exposures. Explicit matrix, holding mean load and geometry fixed: room/dry/reference cyclic load baseline; steady chilled/dry/reference load; service thermal cycling/dry/reference load; room/condensation/reference load; room/dry/reference load plus vibration; combined service thermal cycling/condensation/vibration. Measure cycles to initiation, crack growth, origin, path, fracture appearance and coating damage. Proposed go/no-go: proceed only if the best-tested configuration reproduces the field location and fracture features under service-matched chilled conditions, and controlled contrasts support a testable causal explanation linked to a manufacturing change. No isolated-factor effect would narrow, not end, the mechanism search. Defer if fixtures, environmental control or evidence preservation are infeasible.
- Negotiate a staged timeline: two weeks for permissions, evidence review and capability checks, followed by four weeks for service monitoring and specimen planning; schedule testing only after shared-equipment slots are confirmed. Agree specimen counts, replication, uncertainty reporting and a test-duration ceiling before fabrication; these are planning targets, not commitments.
- After mechanism matching, test welding, filler or geometry changes individually against the unchanged joint under the best field-matching exposure. As a negotiation target, seek at least 25% greater median cycles to matching crack initiation without a new failure mode. Compare all tested configurations on that metric, then propose field monitoring to assess whether warranty failures actually decline.
