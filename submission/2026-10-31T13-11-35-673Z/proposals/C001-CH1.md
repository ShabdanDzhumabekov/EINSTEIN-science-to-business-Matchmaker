# Explainable, Site-Specific Anomaly Detection for ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec reports several terabytes of client captures collected over three years, no labelled malicious traffic, and uncertainty about historical compromise. These archives are therefore unlabelled, not verified clean. [F1]
- ProtoSense inspects Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP. ArmaturaSec reports that false positives from its earlier classifier led clients to disable alerts. [F2]
- The company reports hardware for on-device inference, custom Yocto Linux, and C and Rust firmware components. Its hardware plan discusses anomaly detection alongside packet-forwarding latency. [F3]
- ArmaturaSec identifies one to two part-time engineers as its collaborative staffing capacity and lists traffic archives, a hardware bench, and its firmware codebase as project resources. [F4]
- Kiss expresses openness to joint projects with ICS monitoring companies. Her group operates an ICS testbed, and her notes address industrial access through a data-sharing agreement. [F5]
- Kiss's Modbus study constructed per-register baselines from timing, payload values, and function-code sequences. Her ICAROS project developed calibration procedures without labelled attack data and incremental updates for operational changes. [F6]
- Her Isolation Forest study reports that a tenfold contamination-estimate mismatch reduced average precision from 0.91 to 0.63, documenting a calibration risk rather than deployment-independent reliability. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The pilot could establish whether understandable, site-specific alerts are operationally manageable on ProtoSense, while identifying BACnet, calibration, and hardware obstacles before deployment.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permissions, Kiss could investigate cross-protocol transfer and drift-safe calibration using long-term client captures, controlled scenarios, and target-appliance measurements.

## Our inference
- Site-specific statistical deviations could offer a practical starting point and plain-language explanations. BACnet transfer, new-site performance, and ProtoSense resource fit remain unvalidated; results from other protocols or processors do not establish them.
- Incremental updates could accommodate legitimate operational drift, but might also absorb malicious behaviour. Calibration sensitivity makes operator-gated updates and contamination checks important risks to test.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional: Kiss's availability and allocated effort are unknown, despite her stated openness to this format.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose an eight-week pilot using representative Modbus and BACnet archive subsets as presumed normal, with separate chronological training, tuning, and held-out evaluation periods; check independence and leakage. Compare fixed-threshold rules, site-specific statistical baselines, and incrementally updated one-class models, trained and calibrated without attack labels. Select the best configuration on tuning data, then lock it. Evaluate chronological replay and independently scripted isolated-bench attacks and benign changes, including BACnet transfer and new-client-site adaptation. Proposed go/no-go targets, separately for each protocol on held-out client-site periods: at least 80% detection of injected episodes within 60 seconds, at most one false-or-unresolved alert episode per observed site-day using the review procedure below, and correct plain-language interpretation of at least 80% of explanations by participating plant managers. Also require agreed CPU/RAM limits, viable Yocto/C/Rust integration, and no added forwarding latency under representative sustained and peak traffic. Insufficient evaluation data or uncertain compliance means no pass, regardless of which configuration wins.
- Propose merging alerts for the same site, device, and deviation when separated by at most five minutes. Review every resulting episode; count false and unresolved episodes conservatively against total observed site-days, reporting an upper 95% confidence bound for acceptance. Independently sample 10% of all fixed evaluation windows uniformly, with and without alerts, for expert labelling. These window labels audit missed anomalies, not episode-rate numerators; estimate window totals by inverse-probability weighting. Measure detection only on injected or confirmed cases.
- Propose weeks one–two for permissions, NDA, testbed data-sharing agreement, sampling and hardware limits; weeks three–five for tuning; and weeks six–eight for locked evaluation. Within the stated part-time capacity, company engineers could prepare captures, integrate firmware, and run device benchmarks; Kiss could lead modelling and evaluation design. Test frozen versus operator-approved updates against identical benign changes and injected attacks, with rollback. BACnet bench capability, reviewers, and effort remain prerequisites.
