# Reducing Serbian Support-Search Misses Without Full Re-indexing

## Verified facts
- LinguaBot reports that about 18% of 12,000 real support queries from one bank client returned no useful result. Its analysis attributed more than half of those misses to morphology or script confusion, including within-word script mixing. [F1]
- The company currently uses Elasticsearch with a Croatian-built basic stemmer. It reports that manually maintaining synonym lists does not scale with quarterly product terminology additions. [F2]
- LinguaBot reports around 85,000 labelled query-response pairs across all clients and two in-house linguists. Its stated project parameters include a below-5% miss-rate target, vocabulary updates without full re-indexing, around EUR 8,000 for tooling or data work, and a six-month testing horizon. [F3]
- Dragić's research directly examines interactions between morphological reduction and script normalization. His phoneme-based canonicalization work was evaluated on legal query-document pairs, not LinguaBot support queries. [F4]
- His work also investigates query-side transliteration expansion. Earlier enterprise-search pilots identified terminology-related module updates as a continuing limitation. [F5]
- His published production-load study reports an accuracy–latency tradeoff: the transformer configuration achieved higher MRR but added 47 ms at the 99th percentile. These measurements came from a Serbian news-search workload. [F6]
- Dragić lists openness to company joint projects. His notes describe two active projects, capacity for one smaller industrial engagement, and a preference against general software development without a research component. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could identify which components reduce real support-search failures and whether vocabulary updates remain practical without rebuilding the index.
- For the researcher: Conditional on agreement and permitted access, Dragić could test morphology–script interactions on support-language material and investigate where established retrieval methods fail to transfer.

## Our inference
- This suggests a focused joint research project rather than a platform replacement. The proposal is conditional because Dragić's committed availability is unknown and his stated capacity is limited to a smaller engagement.
- Legal and news retrieval results do not establish performance on short banking queries, within-word typos, or product identifiers. Normalization alone may leave enough unrelated failures to miss the overall target.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a first experiment using permitted support pairs and corresponding knowledge-base documents, with separate tuning and held-out samples whose independence is checked. Compare the current Elasticsearch baseline, Serbian morphological reduction alone, token-level script normalization alone, and both together, holding other settings fixed; tune operation order separately. Select the best configuration on tuning data, whichever it is. Linguists would judge every evaluation query at an agreed retrieval depth, including successful and unsuccessful returns. Define miss rate as queries with no useful result divided by all evaluated queries, without deduplication; count unresolved judgments as misses. Evaluate separately on held-out queries from the original bank, across clients, and after a simulated vocabulary update, reporting inflection and within-word-mixing strata. Proposed go/no-go: each population's 95% upper confidence bound is below 5%; Elasticsearch integration and updates work without full re-indexing; agreed latency and maintenance caps hold; tooling/data costs fit approximately EUR 8,000; and testing finishes within six months. Insufficient evidence means no pass.
- Propose month one for permissions, metric agreement and data splitting; months two–three for the component comparison; and months four–six for held-out evaluation and update replay. Dragić would propose normalization and morphology variants; LinguaBot engineers would implement the Elasticsearch adapter, while its linguists validate relevance and error categories, subject to confirmed capacity.
- Replay newly introduced vocabulary against existing indexed content, testing query-time expansion and incremental mechanisms rather than assuming document insertion solves changed canonical forms. Record update person-hours, regression misses, latency percentiles and resource use. Agree numeric maintenance and latency caps before testing; inspect term collisions, foreign names and alphanumeric products explicitly.
