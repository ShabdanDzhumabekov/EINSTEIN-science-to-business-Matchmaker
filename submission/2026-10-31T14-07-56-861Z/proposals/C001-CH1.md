# Explainable, Site-Specific Anomaly Detection for ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec reports several terabytes of client captures collected over three years, but no confirmed malicious captures. The company acknowledges that historical compromises could have gone unnoticed; these archives are therefore unlabelled, not verified clean traffic. [F1]
- ProtoSense passively inspects Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP. ArmaturaSec reports alert disabling after excessive false positives; its stated problem concerns site-specific behaviour and plant-manager-facing communication. [F2]
- ArmaturaSec reports on-device inference hardware, a custom Yocto environment, and C/Rust firmware. Its hardware plan describes a dedicated security processor associated with anomaly detection without forwarding-latency impact, rather than confirming that processor's current availability. [F3]
- The company describes NDA-governed access to archives, its bench, and firmware. Kiss describes university testbed access under a data-sharing agreement. [F4]
- ArmaturaSec states a collaboration capacity of one to two part-time engineers. [F5]
- Kiss's Modbus research estimated register-level timing, payload, and command-sequence distributions. Her long-term traffic study highlights the risk of training on short captures. [F6]
- Kiss lists openness to joint projects with ICS monitoring companies. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could establish whether site-specific detection reduces operational alert burden while remaining understandable and feasible on current ProtoSense hardware, with separate evidence for BACnet.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement, Kiss could test how statistical baselines transfer across protocols and operating regimes using authorised operational captures, controlled scenarios and appliance measurements.

## Our inference
- Site-specific statistical deviations could provide an interpretable alternative to the company's unsuccessful supervised approach, but operational usefulness remains unproven.
- Modbus evidence suggests a starting method, not validation for BACnet, ArmaturaSec sites, or ProtoSense hardware. Contaminated archives and adaptation could conceal suspicious activity; benign changes could recreate alert fatigue.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional because Kiss's availability and staffing commitment are unknown, despite stated openness to this format.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a six-week passive-replay and bench experiment. ArmaturaSec would supply authorised captures, parser outputs, current hardware and integration effort; Kiss would lead features, calibration and evaluation. Compare fixed rules with site-specific statistical baselines, training and calibrating without attack examples. Use separate chronological training, tuning and held-out periods; check independence and operating-mode coverage. Freeze the tuning-selected configuration before evaluation; evaluation injections cannot inform selection. Measure detection, alert burden, comprehension, CPU, RAM and forwarding latency. Proposed go/no-go for the best configuration, whichever wins: separately for each protocol, at least 90% detection within 60 seconds in every agreed scenario, at most one alert episode per observed site-day, and at least 80% successful managers under the rubric below. Merge alerts for the same site, protocol and entity separated by at most 60 seconds; divide episode counts by fully observed monitoring hours/24. Passing also requires passive operation, agreed current-device resource limits and no forwarding-latency increase within agreed measurement resolution. Missing exposure, scenarios, permissions or evidence means no pass.
- Before evaluation, agree and freeze a per-protocol scenario inventory: scans, replay, unauthorised writes and timing deviations where applicable, alongside legitimate maintenance and load changes. Define independent repetitions, counts, intensity levels and equal scenario weighting. Define each injected episode as one bounded execution, onset as its first injected packet, and a match as an alert identifying the affected entity and deviation within 60 seconds. Keep evaluation injections separate from tuning; unavailable BACnet scenarios remain an unresolved transfer prerequisite.
- Pre-agree a randomly selected explanation sample stratified across both protocols, injected deviations and legitimate changes; every manager assesses the same sample. Score each paraphrase correct only if it identifies the affected entity, changed behaviour and uncertainty without asserting an unsupported attack diagnosis. Define manager success as at least 80% correct in each protocol stratum. Freeze sample sizes, rubric and adjudication before testing; insufficient participation prevents acceptance. Initially freeze baseline updates to avoid silently absorbing suspicious activity.

## Open questions
- Can Kiss confirm availability and staffing for this joint project, and can both parties agree a six-week scope, budget and integration responsibilities compatible with one to two part-time company engineers?
- Which current appliance, CPU/RAM limits, peak traffic loads and forwarding-latency measurement resolution will define acceptance, without assuming the planned processor is available?
- Can authorised reviewers assess every alert episode and a simple random 10% sample of all evaluation windows, including non-alert windows, treating unresolved labels conservatively as potentially missed anomalies and never using sampled windows to estimate episode counts; what minimum exposure and scenario repetitions provide sufficient evidence before acceptance?
- Can the parties approve NDA and data-sharing arrangements, client permissions, independent evaluation captures, BACnet bench capability, representative managers and operator-controlled baseline-update safeguards before starting?
