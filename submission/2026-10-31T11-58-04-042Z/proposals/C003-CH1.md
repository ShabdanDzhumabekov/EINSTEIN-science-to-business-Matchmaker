# Explaining and reducing chilled-conveyor weld cracking

## Verified facts
- AutomVoj describes painted mild-steel hollow-section frames cracking at crossmember-to-stringer welds. It reports retaining two failed sections and site photographs and measurements; access to these materials remains to be arranged. [F1]
- The company reports failures after about 18 months in cold stores typically operating at 2–6 °C, despite passing room-temperature load tests. Thermal cycling, condensation and loaded-belt vibration are its suspected combined explanation, not an established mechanism. [F2]
- AutomVoj lacks low-temperature cyclic-testing equipment and know-how. Erić’s shared chamber and fatigue machine require advance scheduling, so neither immediate access nor a campaign slot is established. [F3]
- AutomVoj has no dedicated R&D department; its chief engineer and two senior automation engineers divide development effort between live projects and improvements. [F4]
- Erić’s published work compares isothermal and thermo-mechanical fatigue programmes, while his field work constructs assessments from measured stress spectra and temperature records. These provide methods for designing a service-informed comparison. [F5]
- Erić states openness to joint projects on low-temperature or combined-loading fatigue. His group routinely uses servo-hydraulic fatigue machines and a climate chamber reaching −60 °C; this does not establish fixture compatibility or condensation-control capability. [F6]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The comparison could distinguish service-related contributors from fabrication issues and establish a defensible test for proposed changes, rather than relying on passing room-temperature tests.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and access, Erić could test whether thermo-mechanical fatigue methods transfer to mildly chilled, painted conveyor joints using field failure evidence and controlled interaction experiments.

## Our inference
- This suggests a relevant joint project, conditional on confirming Erić’s currently unknown availability and allocating company engineering time.
- Transfer remains unvalidated: substantially colder tests on other steels and joint geometries do not establish behaviour of painted hollow sections at 2–6 °C. Condensation and coating effects may require additional corrosion expertise.
- Fabrication defects or unmeasured loads could dominate instead. Accelerating an 18-month failure could change its mechanism; absence of a single-factor effect would narrow, not close, the investigation.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a first comparison using representative painted production crossmember-to-stringer joints, with AutomVoj supplying specimens and service records and Erić developing testing and fracture analysis, subject to agreement. Compare a room-temperature workshop control with an eight-condition chilled matrix: baseline, thermal cycling alone, condensation alone, vibration alone, every pair, and all three. Keep static load, fabrication and coating comparable; define factor levels independently from measured service histories, with a fixed chilled baseline within 2–6 °C. Propose three specimens per condition, recording strain, temperature, wetness, cycles to cracking, crack location and fracture morphology. Select the best-reproducing configuration using tuning specimens. Proposed go/no-go: independent confirmation reproduces the observed connection location and field fracture features in at least two of three specimens, and controlled contrasts support a testable explanation. Otherwise revise hypotheses, not declare success. Confirm acceleration preserves morphology; short tests alone cannot establish 18-month durability.
- Before testing, arrange failed-section and record access; inspect cracks and unfailed comparisons; measure site loading, temperature and moisture histories. Confirm chamber, fixture, load and condensation-control suitability and book shared equipment. Propose a two-week scoping phase followed by an eight-week pilot after access is secured, with specimen counts and duration reviewed against measured cycle rates and available resources.
- Use separate tuning and evaluation specimens, checking independence across production batches. If reproduction succeeds, propose one welding, filler or joint-design change at a time. Negotiate a measurable life-improvement target before testing; evaluate the selected change on held-out joints under confirmed service conditions and follow it in service. No laboratory improvement alone would demonstrate elimination of warranty replacements.
