# Reducing Serbian support-search misses without full re-indexing

## Verified facts
- LinguaBot attributes retrieval misses to Serbian inflection and Latin–Cyrillic mixing, including within-word typos. Both problems therefore belong in the proposed evaluation. [F1]
- The company uses Elasticsearch with a Croatian-oriented stemmer. It needs manageable terminology updates without a full re-index; manually maintained synonym lists have not scaled with quarterly product terminology. [F2]
- LinguaBot reports about 18% without useful results among 12,000 real support queries from one bank, not across all clients. Separately, it has around 85,000 labelled query-response pairs across clients and two linguists available for annotation or validation. [F3]
- The company seeks a miss rate below 5% that remains there with manageable maintenance. Its tooling/data-work budget is around EUR 8,000, and it wants something testable within six months; this does not establish a total collaboration budget. [F4]
- Dragić is open to relevant company joint projects, but his notes limit capacity to a small industrial or consulting engagement alongside two active projects. He is not interested in general software development without a research component. [F5]
- Dragić’s work explicitly studies how stemming and lemmatisation interact with script normalization. His phoneme-canonicalization study evaluated legal-corpus query–document pairs, providing a candidate method rather than validation for support queries. [F6]
- The researcher’s evidence identifies relevant risks: deterministic transliteration fails on some loanwords, abbreviations and alphanumeric strings, while earlier enterprise normalization modules still required terminology-related updates. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The experiment could identify which processing changes reduce support-search misses and whether terminology updates can preserve quality without rebuilding the index.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and data permissions, Dragić could test morphology–script interactions on support queries and obtain validated error analyses of terminology changes and within-word script mixing.

## Our inference
- This suggests a bounded joint research project could fit: Dragić could investigate normalization–morphology interactions while LinguaBot leads integration. The proposal remains conditional because his availability, start date and committed effort are unconfirmed.
- Transfer from legal and other document retrieval to short support queries remains unvalidated. Script normalization alone likely cannot resolve inflection, missing knowledge-base answers or all product-name ambiguities; incremental document insertion also does not establish compatibility with vocabulary-rule changes.

## Proposed next steps
- Propose a six-month first experiment using permitted support queries and knowledge-base snapshots. Compare current Elasticsearch against added phoneme normalization alone, Serbian morphology alone, and both together, keeping ranking and content fixed; define factor settings independently and test both processing orders. Select the best configuration on tuning data, then evaluate independently. LinguaBot would supply integration and linguist judgments; Dragić would propose methods and error analysis. Go only if that configuration achieves below 5% “no useful result” on held-out queries from the original bank, using the agreed original metric, both before and after a reserved terminology update; pooled client results cannot substitute. Also require successful inflection and within-word-mixing tests, no full re-index during updates, precision non-regression, and maintenance effort, latency and tooling/data cost within pre-agreed limits around the stated EUR 8,000 scope. Define numerical robustness, maintenance and latency limits before testing. Missing evaluation data or an untestable prototype by month six means no-go, not success.
- Propose month one for permissions, metric reconstruction and sample design, months two–four for controlled comparisons, and months five–six for held-out and update tests. Separate tuning from evaluation and check independence across repeated queries, customers and time. Have linguists judge a random sample of all evaluation queries, including apparent successes; record uncertain judgments separately and distinguish missing content from retrieval errors.
- Reserve unfamiliar product terms and real within-word mixed-script examples before tuning. Test old and new queries against an unchanged index after terminology-rule updates; report miss rate, precision, tail latency and maintenance hours by failure category and client. Examine product-term collisions and unresolved interactions even when one factor has no isolated effect. Treat deployment and maintenance thresholds as negotiation targets, not established performance.
