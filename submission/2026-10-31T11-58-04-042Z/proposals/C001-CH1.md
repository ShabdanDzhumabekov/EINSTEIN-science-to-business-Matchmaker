# Site-specific, explainable anomaly detection for ProtoSense

## Verified facts
- ArmaturaSec reports no malicious examples and acknowledges possible undetected compromise; its archives therefore cannot be assumed clean. ProtoSense covers Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP, and the company wants baselines that learn each site's behaviour. [F1]
- The company reports that excessive deployment false positives led clients to disable alerts. It also requires explanations understandable to a plant manager. [F2]
- ArmaturaSec says it has hardware for on-device inference and uses custom Yocto Linux. Its hardware-variant plan describes a dedicated security processor intended to support anomaly detection without affecting packet-forwarding latency. [F3]
- ArmaturaSec offers one to two part-time engineers and NDA-governed access to archives, its hardware bench and firmware codebase. Use of Kiss's group testbed requires a data-sharing agreement. [F4]
- Kiss's Modbus research estimated register-level distributions of request timing, payload values and function-code sequences. Her ICAROS project developed threshold calibration without labelled attack data, supporting these methods as experimental starting points. [F5]
- Kiss's incremental Modbus study reports a planned process reconfiguration appearing as a persistent anomaly episode, illustrating a risk of legitimate changes generating sustained alerts. [F6]
- Kiss lists openness to joint projects with companies deploying ICS monitoring solutions. [F7]

## Potential benefits
- For the company: The pilot could establish whether site-specific detection offers an acceptable alert burden and understandable warnings on current hardware, with a separate BACnet transfer check.
- For the researcher: Subject to agreement and permissions, Kiss could test calibration and baseline adaptation across operational captures and controlled scenarios, investigating transfer from Modbus methods to BACnet.

## Our inference
- Site-specific statistical baselines could address the mismatch between public datasets and client operations while explaining alerts through timing, value or command deviations. An anomaly would indicate unusual behaviour, not establish malicious intent.
- Transfer to BACnet and compatibility with current ProtoSense hardware remain unvalidated. Published results on other protocols or devices cannot establish either; the planned processor should not be assumed available.
- This joint-project proposal is conditional because Kiss's availability and allocated team capacity are unknown. A narrow replay-and-bench pilot would likely fit the stated company staffing better than immediate production integration.

## Proposed next steps
- Run a proposed six-week replay-and-bench experiment on site-specific, unlabelled Modbus and BACnet captures. Separate chronological training, tuning and held-out evaluation samples; verify independence and operating-mode coverage. Compare fixed-threshold rules with register/object statistical baselines, trained and calibrated without attack labels. Select configurations using tuning data only. Randomly sample all evaluation windows, including non-alert windows, for expert labelling; estimate false-alert rates from labelled windows and count uncertain cases separately. Measure detection only on isolated injected or confirmed cases, including benign process changes. Proposed go/no-go targets for the best configuration, regardless of method, are at most one false alert/site/day, at least 80% scenario detection within 60 seconds, and plant managers correctly interpreting at least 80% of sampled explanations. Require separate held-out checks for each protocol and site; unavailable evaluation data blocks a pass. Also require local Yocto operation within agreed resource limits and no measurable forwarding-latency increase under agreed traffic loads. All targets are negotiable hypotheses.
- Subject to agreement, Kiss would lead feature design, calibration and evaluation; ArmaturaSec engineers would prepare captures, integrate replay instrumentation and measure CPU, memory, inference time and forwarding latency on currently available hardware. Use weeks one–two for permissions and samples, three–four for tuning, and five–six for locked evaluation, contingent on capacity and BACnet bench availability.
- Before experimentation, agree NDA and testbed data-sharing terms, review responsibilities and evaluation definitions. Test operator-gated baseline updates against benign reconfiguration and injected anomalous traffic, checking whether adaptation suppresses meaningful warnings. Keep automatic updates disabled until reviewed; document rollback and firmware-maintenance ownership.

## Open questions
